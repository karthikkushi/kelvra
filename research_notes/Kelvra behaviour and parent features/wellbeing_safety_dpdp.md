# Wellbeing features, AI-mentor safety and India's children's-data law for an exam-prep study app (research notes, as of 5 Nov 2026)

Method note: 15 tool calls (web search plus fetch). Several compliance-tracker sites (dpdpa.dcomply.in, dpdprules.org, dpdp.myndsolution.com) could not be reached from this sandbox, so the DPDP Rules text was cross-checked from law-firm and news summaries, not from the Gazette PDF. Items marked **[training knowledge, not re-verified]** come from well-known literature with DOI links. They are believed accurate, but no source was fetched for them in this session.

## (a) Which wellbeing features in a study app have evidence and are safe?

### Takeaway
Brief, low-dose, self-administered "mindset" and reappraisal content has the strongest evidence that it can be delivered digitally at scale, especially "stress can be enhancing" combined with growth mindset (Yeager et al. 2022). Expressive writing before exams has mixed replication. Sleep protection (8 to 10 hours for 13 to 18 year olds) and normal breaks are safe, uncontroversial things to recommend. Mood check-ins are reasonable but create a duty to respond to crisis signals. Effects are small. The best-supported content helps students whose starting mindset is negative, and it must not be sold as treatment.

### Cited Findings
**Test anxiety: size of the problem**
- von der Embse, Jester, Roy & Post (2018), *J. Affective Disorders*: a 30-year meta-analysis of 238 studies (1988 onwards). Test anxiety shows small-to-moderate negative associations with performance. Self-esteem was a strong predictor of test anxiety — [PubMed 29156362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29156362/)
- Later work reports that test performance relates more strongly to the cognitive part of test anxiety (worry) than to the emotional part, which supports targeting worry and appraisals — [Frontiers in Psychology 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1401467/full)

**Stress reappraisal / synergistic mindsets**
- Yeager et al. (2022), *Nature* 607:512–520, "A synergistic mindsets intervention protects adolescents from stress": six double-blind randomised studies, N > 4,000, using a self-administered online module of about 30 minutes that combined growth mindset with "stress can be enhancing". It produced less negative appraisals of academic stressors, better cardiovascular responses to stress, lower cortisol and some improvement in wellbeing. The combination beat either component alone, and effects were largest for students who started with negative mindsets. The authors call for larger studies in more diverse populations — [NICHD summary](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/news/070622-teen-stress); [RePEc record, doi 10.1038/s41586-022-04907-7](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v607y2022i7919d10.1038_s41586-022-04907-7.html); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/07/220706153053.htm)
- Jamieson, Mendes, Blackstock & Schmader (2010), *JESP*: telling students that arousal can aid performance before a practice GRE improved math scores **[training knowledge, not re-verified]** — [doi 10.1016/j.jesp.2009.08.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2009.08.015)

**Expressive writing before exams**
- Ramirez & Beilock (2011), *Science* 331:211–213: ninth graders randomly assigned to write about their exam worries for about 10 minutes before a high-stakes exam outperformed controls, with the largest gains in highly test-anxious students — [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/01/110113141605.htm); [doi 10.1126/science.1199427](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199427) **[DOI from training knowledge]**
- Replication is mixed. A published *Science* letter argued that writing about worries could harm students with low test anxiety (the authors replied). A college-classroom test (Myers, Davis & Chan 2021, *Cognitive Research: Principles & Implications*) examined expressive writing as a classroom intervention. An MTSU thesis found little effect for underprepared students — [Myers et al. 2021, PMC8192598](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8192598); [MTSU thesis](https://jewlscholar.mtsu.edu/handle/mtsu/4286)
- **[training knowledge, not re-verified]** Myers et al. (2021) reported no reliable benefit of expressive writing on classroom exam performance. Treat this as unconfirmed until read.

**Growth mindset at scale and its limits [training knowledge, not re-verified]**
- National Study of Learning Mindsets: Yeager et al. (2019), *Nature* 573:364–369. A less-than-1-hour online growth-mindset session given to about 12,000 US 9th graders raised GPA by about 0.10 grade points among lower-achieving students, with effects depending on school peer norms — [doi 10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y)
- Sisk et al. (2018), *Psychological Science*: meta-analysis effect of growth-mindset interventions on achievement d ≈ 0.08 overall, with larger effects for low-SES and at-risk students — [doi 10.1177/0956797617739704](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704). Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023, *Psych. Bulletin*) argue that the effects shrink further when study quality is accounted for. This is still debated.

**Sleep, breaks, activity**
- The AASM consensus says teenagers aged 13 to 18 should regularly get 8 to 10 hours of sleep per 24 hours. Regularly getting less is associated with more self-harm, suicidal thoughts and suicide attempts — [AASM teen sleep advisory](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/teen-sleep-duration-recommendation.pdf)
- An Indian government NHSRC report (2025) on Delhi school adolescents aged 12 to 18 links sleep deprivation with cognitive function. Adolescents with depression were about 3.5 times more likely to report sleep deprivation — [NHSRC report](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/Report%20on%20Prevalence%20of%20Sleep%20Deprivation%20and%20its%20association%20with%20cognitive%20functions%20in%20school%20going%20adolescents%20%2812-18%20Years%29%20in%20Delhi.pdf)
- Sleep disturbance is the top complaint on Tele-MANAS (14% of calls), followed by sadness (14%), stress (11%) and anxiety (9%). The service had handled 14 lakh+ calls since its October 2022 launch (news report) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fdisturbed-sleep-mood-top-list-of-complaints-on-indias-mental-health-helpline-3228979)
- Sleep-dependent memory consolidation and the cost of all-nighters: Okano et al. (2019, *npj Science of Learning*) found that sleep quality, duration and consistency in the weeks before exams predicted grades **[training knowledge, not re-verified]** — [doi 10.1038/s41539-019-0055-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0055-z)

**Mood check-ins / crisis routing (India)**
- Tele-MANAS is India's national tele-mental-health line, 24x7, at **14416** or **1-800-891-4416**, in about 20 languages — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/pdf/1791634745.pdf)
- KIRAN (1800-599-0019), the MoSJE helpline launched in 2020, is reported to have been folded into Tele-MANAS **[training knowledge, not verified; confirm before listing in-app]**.
- Supreme Court, *Sukdeb Saha v. State of Andhra Pradesh*, 2025 INSC 893 (25 July 2025): 15 binding interim guidelines for **all** educational institutions, explicitly including coaching institutes. They include a uniform mental-health policy based on UMMEED, MANODARPAN and the National Suicide Prevention Strategy, and they stay in force until legislation is enacted. They run in parallel with the National Task Force on student mental health, which was constituted on 24 March 2025 and is chaired by Justice S. Ravindra Bhat — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/app-lite/court-updates/supreme-court/sukdeb-saha-v-the-state-of-andhra-pradesh-2025-insc-893-mental-health-policy-educational-institutions-1586163); [CMHLP analysis](https://cmhlp.org/blogs/a-landmark-ruling-for-student-mental-health-in-india/); [LiveLaw on NTF](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/supreme-court-national-task-force-and-student-mental-health-307933); [JNU compliance circular Oct 2025](https://www.jnu.ac.in/sites/default/files/circular_ComplianceHon'bleSupremeCourtofIndiaJudgment_14Oct2025.pdf)
- A January 2026 ruling (*Amit Kumar v. Union of India*) issued further directions to higher-education institutions based on the NTF's recommendations, referring back to *Sukdeb Saha* — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/supreme-court-national-task-force-and-student-mental-health-307933) (as summarised in search results; details not read)
- Reported elements of *Sukdeb Saha* include displaying helpline numbers (Tele-MANAS and others) prominently, written protocols for immediate referral, and no segregation of students by performance or public shaming. The 2-month and 90-day deadlines quoted by some sites are inconsistent across sources — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-07-26/social-issues/supreme-court-issues-guidelines-to-address-student-suicides-mental-health-in-educational-institutions); [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/?p=18718)
- MoE *Guidelines for Regulation of Coaching Centre 2024* (January 2024): no enrolment under 16 (or before the secondary school exam), no misleading promises of ranks or marks, classes without undue pressure, counselling for students. These are **model** guidelines that states must adopt through their own law — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/cannot-enrol-students-below-16-years-no-misleading-promises-moe-guidelines-for-coaching-centres); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/moe-guidelines-for-coaching-centres/print_manually)

### Inferences
- **Safe and supported to ship:** a single 20 to 30 minute "stress can fuel you + abilities grow" module (Yeager-style), with short reappraisal reminders before mock tests; an *optional* 10-minute "write your worries" exercise before big exams, worded as optional because of the low-anxiety caveat; sleep-protection nudges (no late-night streak pressure, wind-down reminders, framing "sleep is when memory consolidates"); Pomodoro-style breaks and suggestions to move.
- **Do not:** gamify all-nighters or late study, use rank-shaming or leaderboards that segregate by performance (this conflicts with the spirit of the *Sukdeb Saha* directions), or claim clinical effects.
- Mood check-ins should be opt-in, low frequency, and wired to a crisis pathway (Tele-MANAS 14416) before launch. Collecting mood data from minors may also count as "behavioural monitoring" under DPDP s.9(3) (see (c)).
- Self-compassion: I found no fetched source in this session. Neff-style self-compassion meta-analyses exist, but their evidence for exam outcomes in adolescents needs a separate check.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for brief mindfulness in students were not retrieved (e.g., Dunning et al. 2019 *JCPP* meta-analysis of mindfulness in youth, which reported small effects; not verified here).
- Myers et al. 2021 results were not read. No dedicated meta-analysis of pre-exam expressive writing was found.
- No sleep-deprivation/adolescent-memory meta-analysis was retrieved.
- No evidence was found on harms of ecological momentary assessment (mood tracking) in teens, such as rumination or reactivity. This is a known open question.
- The full text of the *Sukdeb Saha* directions was not read, so the specific requirements for edtech or online platforms are unconfirmed. The guidelines name "coaching institutes", and whether they reach a pure app is untested.

## (b) Risks of AI companions/mentors for teens and recommended safeguards

### Takeaway
Between 2024 and 2026, regulators and courts treated emotionally engaging chatbots as a child-safety risk. The record includes the Character.AI wrongful-death suits (settled January 2026), Character.AI banning open-ended chat for under-18s (25 November 2025), the FTC's 6(b) study of seven companion-bot firms (11 September 2025), California's SB 243 (in force 1 January 2026), OpenAI's parental controls with distress alerts (September 2025, expanded July 2026), and the APA health advisory (June 2025). Across these, the consensus safeguards are: disclose AI status, no romantic or sexual roleplay with minors, crisis detection and escalation to humans and helplines, limits against dependency, age assurance with safer defaults when age is uncertain, and parental tools.

### Cited Findings
- **FTC:** on 11 September 2025 the FTC issued Section 6(b) orders to seven operators of consumer "companion" chatbots (including Google, Character.AI, Meta, Snap and OpenAI). The orders ask about safety testing, impacts on children, monetisation of engagement, and disclosures. This is a study, not enforcement — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots); [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/ftc-probes-ai-companion-chatbots-for-risks-to-minors/)
- **California SB 243** (signed 13 October 2025, effective 1 January 2026) is the first US "companion chatbot" law. It requires clear disclosure that the user is talking to AI, safeguards, and a private right of action. Reported provisions include suicide/self-harm protocols with referral to crisis services, break reminders for known minors, preventing sexual content with minors, and annual reporting to the Office of Suicide Prevention. **Verify against the enacted text**: some summaries may describe the introduced version. Newsom vetoed the broader AB 1064 — [California Lawyers Assn](https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/regulatory-focus-on-ai-companion-character-chatbots/); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ai-chatbots-face-rising-legal-and-legislative-scrutiny)
- **Character.AI:** after lawsuits including *Garcia* (the 14-year-old Sewell Setzer's suicide, 2024), the company banned open-ended chat for under-18s from 25 November 2025 and added third-party age assurance. In January 2026 Character.AI and Google settled suits in Florida, New York, Colorado and Texas (terms undisclosed) — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/07/google-character-ai-lawsuits-teen-suicides); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/08/google-character-ai-settle-lawsuits-teenage-child-suicides-chatbots); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/character-ai-google-agree-to-settle-teen-chatbot-harm-lawsuits)
- Reports that Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 and introduced mandatory face-based age verification in April 2026 come from lower-quality sources — **unconfirmed** — [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/character-ai-chapter-11-bankruptcy-teen-chatbot-safety-479913); [futureskills](https://futureskills.academy/lab/incidents/character-ai-minors-ban-and-settlements/)
- **OpenAI:** on 16 September 2025 OpenAI announced age prediction, defaulting to the under-18 experience when age is uncertain. Parental controls launched on 29 September 2025: account linking, quiet or blackout hours, turning off memory and chat history, and notifications to parents when a teen shows signs of acute distress, with possible law-enforcement contact if parents are unreachable. A 13 July 2026 update expanded parent notifications. Age prediction was still described as in development — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/29/chatgpt-openai-parental-controls); [OpenAI parental controls](https://openai.com/pl-PL/index/introducing-parental-controls/); [OpenAI age prediction](https://openai.com/hu-HU/index/building-towards-age-prediction/)
- The *Raine v. OpenAI* suit (August 2025, a 16-year-old's suicide) prompted these changes **[training knowledge; not retrieved in this session]**.
- **APA Health Advisory, "Artificial intelligence and adolescent well-being"** (3 June 2025), covering ages 10 to 25. Concerns: unhealthy attachment to chatbots, teens not realising they are talking to AI, and teens being less likely to question a bot's accuracy or intent. Recommendations: healthy boundaries in simulated human relationships; age-appropriate privacy defaults, interaction limits and content; transparency, human oversight and rigorous testing; prominent notice that AI health information does not replace professionals; support for beneficial uses such as brainstorming and summarising — [APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-ai-adolescent-well-being); [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2025/06/protect-adolescent-ai-users)
- UNICEF *Policy Guidance on AI for Children* (v2.0, 2021) sets nine requirements, including prioritising fairness and non-discrimination, protecting children's data and privacy, ensuring safety, and providing transparency and accountability **[training knowledge, not re-verified; check whether an updated edition was issued in 2025]** — [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children)

### Inferences
- An AI *study mentor* sits close to the "companion" category if it builds a persona, remembers personal life details or offers emotional support. Design it as a tutor and coach, not a friend or partner.
- Default to the conservative "under-18 experience" whenever age is unknown, following OpenAI's pattern. India's DPDP treats everyone under 18 as a child, so the same default fits there.
- Parent distress alerts (the OpenAI model) conflict with teen confidentiality and could be dangerous where the family is the stressor. Use a tiered approach: show helplines immediately in-app, offer a warm handoff to Tele-MANAS, and alert a parent only for imminent risk, under a disclosed policy.

### Gaps
- The FTC 6(b) staff report had not been located as of this research, so its status is unknown.
- The text of SB 243 as enacted was not read.
- No India-specific AI-chatbot child-safety regulation was found. MeitY's AI Governance Guidelines (November 2025) were not checked.

## (c) India's legal requirements for children's data in an edtech app (DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025)

### Takeaway
Under the DPDP Act a "child" is anyone under 18. Section 9 requires verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data. It bans processing likely to harm a child's wellbeing and bans **tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children**. The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)). The children's obligations (Rule 10 and s.9) take effect **18 months later, about 13 May 2027**. MeitY's January 2026 proposal to shorten the timeline had **not been notified as of September to November 2026** per trackers. The education exemption (Fourth Schedule) covers **educational institutions** only, for their educational activities or child safety. A commercial edtech app that is not itself an "institution of learning" should assume **no exemption**.

### Cited Findings
**Status and timeline**
- Rules notified 13 November 2025 (some sources say 14 November) as G.S.R. 846(E). Phases: Board and definitional provisions immediately; Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) after 12 months (about 13 November 2026); most substantive duties, including Rules 3 and 5 to 16 (notice, security, breach, retention, **Rule 10 children**, Rule 12 exemptions) and sections 7 to 10 of the Act, after 18 months (about 13 to 14 May 2027) — [matters.ai](https://www.matters.ai/compliance/dpdp/dpdp-rules-2025); [EY India](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/transforming-data-privacy-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025); [PwC India](https://www.pwc.in/research-insights/news_alert/regulatory-insights/meity-notifies-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025.html); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/tech-today/news/story/final-dpdp-rules-2025-notified-global-tech-platforms-to-face-stricter-obligations-higher-penalties-502827-2025-11-19)
- MeitY proposed (January 2026 consultations, comments due 4 February 2026) cutting the 18-month window to 12 months or less, mainly for Significant Data Fiduciaries. MirvoLegal (as at 20 September 2026) reports **no amending notification**. One tracker mentions an October 2026 "Removal of Difficulties Order" that **could not be verified** — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs); [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/); [India AI Rulebook](https://indiaairulebook.com/learn/data-privacy/dpdp-deadlines-2026-2027)

**Act, Section 9 (children)** **[section text from training knowledge; consistent with the law-firm summaries cited]**
- s.2(f): "child" means an individual under 18.
- s.9(1): obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian before processing a child's personal data, in the manner prescribed (Rule 10).
- s.9(2): no processing likely to cause any detrimental effect on a child's well-being. This is **not** exempted by the Fourth Schedule — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)
- s.9(3): no tracking or behavioural monitoring of children and no targeted advertising directed at children.
- s.9(4) to (5): the Government may exempt classes of fiduciaries or purposes (Fourth Schedule), and may lower the age for fiduciaries whose processing is "verifiably safe". **No such age-lowering notification was found.**

**Rule 10: verifiable parental consent**
- The fiduciary must adopt "appropriate technical and organisational measures" so that consent comes from a parent who is an identifiable adult, and must do due diligence. Methods: (i) reliable identity and age details the fiduciary already holds; (ii) details voluntarily provided, or a **virtual token** mapped to identity/age issued by an authorised entity, including a Digital Locker (DigiLocker) service provider. A tick-box is not sufficient. No single method is mandated — [Nishant Munjal explainer](https://nishantmunjal.com/dpdp-childrens-data-verifiable-parental-consent-india/); [Digital Anumati edtech guide](https://trust.digitalanumati.com/verifiable-parental-consent-edtech-india-implementation-guide/); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- Open interpretive issue: the Rules do not say whether parental consent collected earlier without Rule 10 checks remains valid after May 2027. Plan to re-verify — [JurisEagle](https://juriseagle.com/insights/dpdp-children-data-educational-institutions)

**Rule 12 / Fourth Schedule exemptions**
- **Part A** (classes of fiduciaries exempt from s.9(1) and s.9(3)) includes clinical establishments and healthcare professionals; allied healthcare professionals; **educational institutions**, limited to tracking and behavioural monitoring for their educational activities or the safety of children enrolled with them ("educational institution" = an institution of learning imparting education, including vocational education); crèches and day-care (safety only); and school transport (location tracking during travel only) — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/dpdp-rules-carve-out-key-exemptions-for-healthcare-providers-schools-and-childcare-services-processing-childrens-data-84208.htm); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/)
- **Part B** (purpose-based) includes exercising powers under law; providing subsidies or services in the child's interest; **creating a user account for email communication only**; ensuring a child does not access harmful information; and confirming that a user is *not* a child (age verification) — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/?p=47749); [ELP Law PDF, December 2025](https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Processing-of-childrens-personal-data-under-the-DPDP-Act-what-does-it-means-for-businesses.pdf). The full Part B list was compiled partly from training knowledge and should be verified against G.S.R. 846(E).
- Whether a coaching centre or a pure edtech platform counts as an "educational institution" is **contested and untested**. Practitioners advise edtechs not to rely on the exemption — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/); [Tsaaro on edtech](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/teaching-in-the-age-of-dpdpa-when-child-safety-becomes-edtech%E2%80%99s-competitive-advantage)

**Other duties relevant to an edtech app (all from May 2027)** **[mostly training knowledge; cross-check against the Rules]**
- Rule 3: standalone, plain-language notice listing the data and purposes, with a way to withdraw consent. Rule 6: reasonable security, including encryption/masking, access control, and keeping logs for at least 1 year. Rule 7: notify the Board and affected users of a breach without delay, with a detailed report to the Board **within 72 hours**. Rule 8: erasure once the purpose ends; the fixed 3-year inactivity rule applies only to large e-commerce, gaming and social-media platforms, with 48 hours' notice before erasure. s.8(7) and s.6: data minimisation and purpose limitation (consent only for data "necessary for the specified purpose"). s.8(10) and Rule 14: grievance redressal within 90 days.
- **Penalties (Act Schedule):** up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards; up to ₹200 crore for failing to notify a breach; **up to ₹200 crore for breach of the s.9 additional obligations for children**; up to ₹50 crore residual — [LatestLY quoting Govt](https://www.latestly.com/technology/dpdp-act-important-step-in-building-trustworthy-and-future-ready-digital-environment-for-india-says-government-7208060.html); [Treelife](https://treelife.in/tag/dpdp). The ₹200 crore children's penalty was **not confirmed** by a source fetched in this session; it comes from the Act's Schedule (item 3) per training knowledge.

### Inferences
- For the 14 to 17 year olds in a 14 to 24 user base, Kelvra needs Rule 10 verifiable parental consent (DigiLocker token or equivalent) by **13 May 2027**, and should build it now. Users aged 18 to 24 are adults under the DPDP Act and consent for themselves.
- **Behavioural monitoring of minors is the core legal risk for an AI mentor.** Adaptive learning analytics, mood check-ins, "engagement" scoring, streak manipulation, and feeding chat content into personalisation or ads could all be read as "behavioural monitoring" under s.9(3). Kelvra is probably not an "educational institution", so it likely cannot rely on the Part A carve-out. Keep any learner modelling strictly tied to the educational service the parent consented to, document why it is necessary, and never use it for ads or growth loops. This is a legal gray zone, so get counsel's opinion.
- Section 9(2) ("detrimental effect on well-being") is never exempt. Manipulative engagement design, rank-shaming, and companion-style dependency are compliance risks as well as ethical ones.
- Age assurance itself is a Part B exempt purpose, so collecting minimal data to check whether a user is a child does not require parental consent.

### Gaps
- The Gazette text (G.S.R. 846(E)) was not fetched, because the tracker domains were blocked and the PIB release was not found. Exact wording of Rule 10, the illustrations and the Fourth Schedule should be confirmed from egazette.gov.in or meity.gov.in.
- The "Removal of Difficulties Order (October 2026)" is unverified.
- No law-firm view from Cyril Amarchand, Trilegal or AZB was retrieved. Summaries came from Tsaaro, S.S. Rana, KS&K, ELP, PwC and EY.

### Red lines and must-haves checklist: AI study mentor for 14 to 24 year olds in India

**Red lines (never)**
1. No targeted or behavioural advertising to anyone under 18, and no use of chat or mood data for ads (DPDP s.9(3)).
2. No tracking or behavioural profiling of under-18s beyond what is strictly necessary for the consented educational service. No engagement scoring, dark patterns or manipulative streaks (s.9(2) and (3)).
3. No romantic, sexual or "girlfriend/boyfriend" roleplay, and no persona that claims to be human or claims feelings. The AI must never discourage a student from seeking human help (SB 243, APA, Character.AI precedent).
4. No diagnosis, therapy claims or medication advice. No handling of self-harm content without escalation.
5. No rank-shaming, public leaderboards that segregate weak performers, or "guaranteed rank" claims (*Sukdeb Saha*; MoE coaching guidelines 2024).
6. Do not encourage all-nighters or late-night study streaks.
7. Do not process an under-18's data before verifiable parental consent once Rule 10 applies (13 May 2027), and best practice is to comply now.

**Must-haves**
1. Age gate at sign-up. When age is uncertain, default to the under-18 mode.
2. Rule 10 verifiable parental consent flow (DigiLocker or virtual token, or reliable existing identity), with a standalone notice, easy withdrawal and grievance contact (90 days).
3. A visible, persistent "I'm an AI" disclosure, plus periodic break and "talk to a person" reminders.
