## 1. Named examples of solo or 2-3 person products with public revenue (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Real solo revenue clusters at USD 1K-15K MRR and is mostly B2B tools, developer tools, and Chrome extensions sold globally in dollars; the six-figure cases are outliers with an existing audience or viral short video. ### Inferences
- Indian founders with public dollar revenue almost all sell B2B or developer tools to global customers (Orshot, Guidejar, Indie Kit, Mockey, SiteGPT, SalesRobot). I found zero public cases of a solo Indian developer earning comparable recurring software revenue from Indian consumers.
- The India-facing cases that do earn (Delta Education, the non-metro services business) are service-plus-community businesses with small revenue (Rs 2-10 lakh per year), not SaaS.
- Repeat attempts are the norm: Guidejar was a third product; Orshot followed Pika. First product success is rare in this sample.
- A realistic target for a no-audience solo developer in year one is USD 500-5,000 MRR, with Rs 1 lakh per month (USD 1,200) achievable in roughly 5-12 months in the cases that reported timelines (Habit Pixel 8 months to USD 1K; Indie Kit USD 6K total in about 5 months; USD 29 extension USD 20K in about 5 months).
- Every six-figure solo case in this list had either a pre-existing audience (Photo AI, TypingMind, ShipFast) or viral short video (Rayz AI). Neither applies to the reader today.

### Gaps
- Price points for most products were not in snippets (pages could not be opened).
- How first 100 users were found is documented for only about a third of the list.
- No TrustMRR leaderboard data could be pulled directly; only one verified listing (Faceless.so) surfaced.
- No verified revenue for an India-to-India solo software product was found. This absence is itself a signal but may also reflect that Indian founders publish less.
- Indie Hackers posts are increasingly AI-summarised "Fake Mayo" interviews; quality of underlying verification is unknown.

## 2. Selling inside India: what Indians pay for, price points, UPI AutoPay, churn, conversion

### Takeaway
Indian consumers pay at scale for a narrow set of emotionally urgent or entertainment categories (astrology, audio stories, micro-dramas, short skill videos), at very low price points and with heavy paid marketing; these are funded-company games, not solo-budget games. UPI AutoPay makes sign-up easy but renewal is unreliable, so churn is structurally high.

### Cited Findings

**Market size and who can pay**
- Blume's Indus Valley Report 2025 estimates India's "consuming class" at about 140 million people (about 30 million households, roughly 10% of the population); close to 1 billion people lack discretionary spending power — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/india-wealth-gap-consumption-study-2025-blume-125022800468_1.html), [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1079648/scroll_in)
- "Downloads and paying users can be very different things in India" — [Inc42 on microdramas](https://inc42.com/features/indias-microdrama-boom-is-here-but-can-the-unit-economics-work/)

**Categories with demonstrated willingness to pay (all are funded companies, not solo)**
- Astrology: Astrotalk FY25 total income Rs 1,214 crore (up 85% from Rs 656 crore); operating revenue Rs 1,176 crore; adjusted PBT Rs 285 crore. Model is pay-per-minute consultation, about Rs 10-250 per minute; 80% of consultation revenue from returning users (the last two from a GrowthX student case study, not audited) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/news/astrotalk-reports-85-revenue-growth-in-fy25-as-tier-i-cities-boost-platform-activity), [Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/astrotalk-revenue-surges-85-per-cent-in-fy25-as-paid-consultations-soar/), [GrowthX](https://growthx.club/proof-of-work/consumer-services/astrotalk/monetization-project-----astrotalk|685c142bc580e3058feaab3a)
- Audio stories / micro-drama: Kuku FM FY25 operating revenue about Rs 242 crore (up from Rs 87.9 crore), net loss Rs 152.6 crore, marketing spend about Rs 285 crore, claims 1 crore+ paying subscribers — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/kuku-fm-reports-rs-240-cr-revenue-in-fy25-spends-rs-285-cr-on-marketing-10943137), [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/unlisted-companies/ipo-bound-kuku-fm-s-fy25-revenue-jumps-by-almost-3x-to-242-crore-net-loss-widens-to-153-crore)
- Kuku's founder discussed "charging Bharat Rs 399 a year" (podcast title only; details not read) — [The Ken podcast](https://the-ken.com/podcasts/first-principles/kukus-lal-chand-bisu-on-charging-bharat-%e2%82%b9399-a-year-the-bathoth-to-bandra-arc-and-why-nos-beat-yeses/)
- Short skill/"edutainment" video: Seekho FY25 revenue Rs 141.5 crore (12.3x from Rs 11.5 crore) while spending Rs 134 crore on advertising — [Medial/Entrackr](https://medial.app/news/seekho-spends-rs-134-cr-on-advertising-for-rs-142-cr-revenue-in-fy25-4706ea50f7972)

**Price points and trial mechanics seen in the wild**
- Rs 1 or Rs 2 trial that converts into a UPI auto-debit of Rs 149-699; Stage's stated flow is Rs 1 now, Rs 199 per quarter after 7 days, with WhatsApp reminders during the trial. An anonymous complaint to the RBI alleged users are not clearly told; the companies dispute this — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-auto-debit-scam-kuku-fm-stage-paytm-google-pay-phonepe-how-cancel-auto-debit)
- "Much of the economy is sachet-based pricing or prepay" (Lightspeed partner view); roughly half of UPI transactions are small/micropayments (unsourced in the article; OLDER, 2023) — [Afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/pov/small-payments-big-impact-digital-content-companies-adopt-sachet-culture)

**UPI AutoPay behaviour and churn**
- Mint, citing NPCI data: 55-90% of UPI AutoPay debits across banks failed in August 2025; causes include insufficient balance; this is driving involuntary cancellations and pushing some subscription businesses back toward cards — [Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/Mint-New-Delhi/UPI-AUTOPAYS-ENDLESS-WOES-FORCING-AN-INDUSTRY-RETHINK)
- Razorpay claims involuntary churn is nearly 30% of subscriber attrition and that mandate set-ups rose 120% in 2025 (vendor marketing; no independent source) — [Razorpay blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/26567)
- Mandates stay active until the user cancels in the UPI app; many users wrongly assume uninstalling cancels — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-auto-debit-scam-kuku-fm-stage-paytm-google-pay-phonepe-how-cancel-auto-debit)

**Conversion evidence**
- RevenueCat 2025 (via secondary coverage): median download-to-paid conversion within 35 days is 1.4% in India and Southeast Asia versus 2.6% in North America and 2.0% in Western Europe; median year-one realised value per paying user is USD 14 in India/SEA versus USD 32 in North America — [The Social Outline summary](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/download-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks) (page attribution not confirmed), [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Vendor claim, unverified: a USD 9.99/month global price converts at 2-4% in India while a PPP-adjusted price of about USD 2.99 converts at 8-14% — [AppsOps blog](https://appsops.store/blog/revenuecat-data-local-pricing)
- RevenueCat (via coverage): AI apps earn 41% more per user but churn 30% faster — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- "Stripe-only checkout fails in India at high rates"; UPI and Razorpay are needed for domestic sales — [eChai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/first-customers/i-m-building-for-indian-smbs-who-use-whatsapp-for-everything-how-do-i-get-my-first-10-without-a-fancy-sales-process) (page attribution not confirmed)
- For Indian SMBs, WhatsApp Business is the support and sales channel, not ticketing tools — [Bootstrappers.com](https://bootstrappers.com/whatsapp-for-customer-support-not-if-these-founders-can-help-it/)

### Inferences
- Unit maths for a solo India B2C subscription: at about 1.4% install-to-paid and roughly USD 14 (about Rs 1,150-1,200) per payer in year one, reaching Rs 1 lakh per month (Rs 12 lakh per year) needs on the order of 1,000 paying users a year, which implies roughly 70,000+ installs a year. Without paid ads or a viral channel that is very hard. (My arithmetic from the RevenueCat figures above.)
- The categories that work in India (astrology, stories, dramas, quick skills) monetise emotion, entertainment and aspiration, and the winners spend nearly as much on ads as they earn (Seekho Rs 134 crore ads for Rs 142 crore revenue; Kuku Rs 285 crore marketing for Rs 242 crore revenue). A zero-budget solo developer cannot copy the acquisition model, only the pricing model.
- Per-use / pay-per-minute / coin pricing (Astrotalk, Pocket FM) outperforms monthly subscription in India, and sidesteps AutoPay failure. For a solo product, one-time or prepaid packs via plain UPI are safer than recurring mandates.
- Rs 1 trial into auto-debit is under regulatory and reputational pressure; it is risky for a small unknown brand.
- Utility software and productivity tools for Indian consumers did not appear in any evidence of real willingness to pay.

### Gaps
- No audited conversion or churn figures for Rs 1 trials or for Rs 49-299 per month price bands.
- The Blume Indus Valley Report 2026 was not found in search; only the 2025 edition is cited.
- No RedSeer or Bain figures on Indian paying-user counts surfaced.
- No evidence found (either way) on matrimony, English-speaking, devotion, jobs, health or personal-finance apps at solo scale; these categories are known to have large funded players, but I have no sourced numbers from this session.
- Indian SMB (B2B) price points in rupees were not found.

## 3. Selling globally from India: payments, legal, tax

### Takeaway
In 2026 a solo Indian developer generally cannot open Stripe (invite-only since May 2024), so the practical options are a merchant of record (Dodo Payments, or Paddle) for global dollar sales and Razorpay for Indian UPI sales. Software exports are zero-rated under GST if a Letter of Undertaking is filed.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe India has been invite-only for new Indian merchants since May 2024; general availability had not resumed per 2026 guides — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/compare/stripe-alternatives), [Winvesta](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/stripe-alternatives-for-india-best-payment-platforms-for-freelancers-and-businesses)
- Lemon Squeezy (owned by Stripe) inherits the India restriction and in 2026 announced it is migrating merchants to Stripe Managed Payments — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/lemonsqueezy-alternative-for-indian-saas-accepting-global-payments/)
- Dodo Payments: merchant of record aimed at Indian indie founders; no monthly fee; headline fee 4% + USD 0.40 per transaction; its localized pricing pages show India domestic cards/UPI at 4% + USD 0.15; payouts pooled to an Indian bank account, no foreign entity required. Sources conflict on exact fees and most are Dodo's own pages — [Dodo MoR page](https://dodopayments.com/meta/merchant-of-record), [Dodo pricing](https://dodopayments.com/de/pricing), [Karbon review](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/dodo-payment), [Fungies review](https://fungies.io/dodo-payments-review-2026.md)
- Paddle: merchant of record, described as better suited to larger SaaS; cited rate 5% + USD 0.50 (from a Dodo comparison page, unverified) — [Dodo comparison](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/top-5-alternatives-to-lemon-squeezy)
- Razorpay: domestic about 2% + GST; international cards 3% + GST, international bank transfers 1% + GST, plus possible forex mark-ups; individuals and sole proprietors are eligible with a personal PAN; settlement around T+7 for international — [Razorpay pricing explainer](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/), [Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments.md), [Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/razorpay-international-payment-gateway-charges)
- Razorpay is not a merchant of record: the seller remains responsible for foreign sales tax/VAT — [Dodo Razorpay review](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/razorpay-review)
- For invoiced B2B export receipts, Skydo, Payoneer and similar are open to any Indian business with PAN/Aadhaar/GST — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/compare/stripe-alternatives)
- GST: export of services is zero-rated under Section 16 of the IGST Act; "export of services" under Section 2(6) requires supplier in India, recipient outside India, place of supply outside India, and payment in convertible foreign exchange — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-software-saas-exports-international-clients/), [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/software-export-gst-firc-rbi-compliance)
- A GST-registered exporter files a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) each financial year to export without paying IGST; LUT for FY 2026-27 was due before 31 March 2026 — [IncorpX LUT guide](https://www.incorpx.io/guide/how-to-apply-for-gst-lut-export-services), [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/gst-on-saas-exports-lut)
- Proof of foreign inward remittance (FIRC/FIRA) is what supports the export claim and any refund — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/fira-gst-refund-saas-exporters-india)
- A sole proprietor who exported software without filing an LUT appears as an unresolved forum case, showing this is a common slip — [Tax Management India](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/web/tmi_blog_details.asp?id=823381)
- Indian founders earning in dollars: see Section 1A (Orshot USD 6.2K MRR, Guidejar USD 4K MRR, Indie Kit USD 6K total, Mockey USD 12K MRR, SalesRobot USD 1.25M all-time, SiteGPT unverified).

### Inferences
- Practical zero-cost stack for the reader: Dodo Payments (or Paddle) for global checkout because the merchant of record handles overseas VAT/sales tax; Razorpay for Indian UPI customers. Fees of roughly 4-5% plus forex are the price of not needing a foreign company.
- With a merchant of record, the developer's legal customer is the MoR (a foreign entity), so receipts are a B2B export of services; GST registration plus LUT keeps it zero-rated. Whether GST registration is compulsory below the Rs 20 lakh services threshold for a pure exporter is a question for a chartered accountant; I did not find a clear primary source.
- Selling in dollars gives roughly 5-10x the revenue per customer of rupee pricing for the same product (USD 39/month Orshot entry plan versus Rs 99-199 Indian price points), which is the main reason the documented Indian solo successes sell globally.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm current Paddle or Polar onboarding rules for Indian sole proprietors, or PayPal India status, from primary pages.
- Dodo's exact current fee table needs checking on its live pricing page; sources conflict (4% + 40c vs 4% + 15c for India domestic vs an older 5% + 50c).
- No official CBIC source was read; all GST points are from CA-firm and vendor blogs. Income-tax treatment (presumptive taxation under 44ADA/44AD) was not researched.
- A blog claims the new direct tax code may alter "export of services" analysis; unverified, single source — [ITA 2025 converter blog](https://ita1961to2025.pages.dev/blog/digital-nomads-saas/gst-apply-23)

### Takeaway
The channels with the best evidence for a no-audience solo developer are marketplaces with built-in search (Chrome Web Store, app stores, Shopify-type marketplaces), community-first groups (Telegram/WhatsApp), and consistent short video; classic informational SEO is being eroded by AI Overviews and Reddit self-promotion is heavily policed.

- ChatGPT apps / MCP directories are early and uncrowded per vendor (94.6% single-app vendors) but have no proven monetisation path yet; they are a distribution bet, not a revenue channel, as of late 2026.
- The reader's stack (React/Vite/Firebase/Vercel/Groq) is directly suited to free web tools and Chrome extensions, which are the two cheapest proven funnels in this evidence.

## 5. B2B vs B2C: which reaches the first Rs 1 lakh per month faster

### Takeaway
No dataset directly measures time-to-first-USD-1K by segment, but all the indirect evidence favours B2B (or prosumer tools priced like B2B): about double the median revenue, roughly half the churn, and far fewer customers needed. B2C wins only when a viral channel exists.

### Cited Findings
- Median MRR among revenue-earning startups: USD 198 for B2B versus USD 99 for B2C (vendor research on verified-revenue data) — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/b2b-vs-b2c-saas)
- Monthly churn: 5-10% typical for low-touch micro-SaaS versus 2-5% gross for niche B2B micro-SaaS (blog benchmark) — [WinSavvy](https://winsavvy.com/?p=66887)
- A solo B2C founder at about USD 1K MRR after 5 months had 1,700 users, 45 paying users and 65 payments (1.44 payments per payer: "one-and-done") and concluded the maths required a pivot to B2B — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/1k-mrr-1700-users-5-months-solo-and-the-math-just-told-me-to-pivot-to-b2b-3791c8e98c)
- At USD 1K MRR growth is acquisition-limited, and early customers usually come through hand-to-hand outreach rather than scaled channels — [Prompts to Product](https://www.promptstoproduct.com/why-most-saas-dies-between-1k-and-10k-mrr)
- India consumer subscriptions: 1.4% install-to-paid and USD 14 per payer in year one — [RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- B2C can be very fast with viral short video: Dumbphone USD 9K MRR in 78 days; Rayz AI USD 80K MRR in 75 days (self-reported, vendor case studies) — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/20k-downloads-and-9k-mrr-in-78-days-how-this-indie-app-turned-minimalism-into-millions-of-views)
- MicroConf's long-standing guidance favours B2B for bootstrappers — [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/latest/b2b-saas) (content not read; title only)

### Inferences
- Customer-count arithmetic for Rs 1 lakh (USD 1,200) per month: 31 customers at USD 39/month (Orshot's entry price); about 60 at USD 19; about 600 Indian consumers at Rs 199/month with AutoPay failures on top; or 41 one-time sales per month at USD 29. Thirty to sixty business customers can be found by hand; six hundred consumers cannot without a channel.
- For this reader the faster path is global B2B/prosumer (dollars, 30-60 customers, manual outreach plus one marketplace), not Indian B2C subscriptions. Indian B2C is viable only as pay-per-use in a high-emotion category with an owned community channel.
- A third option the evidence supports for India: sell a service to Indian small businesses first (the non-metro founder's 82% margin services; Delta Education), then productise.

### Gaps
- No cohort data (e.g., ProfitWell/Baremetrics) on months to first USD 1K MRR by B2B vs B2C.
- No data on Indian SMB software churn or sales-cycle length for a solo seller.

## 6. Common failure patterns of solo AI products

### Takeaway
The dominant failure is not bad code but no buyers: products ship, some traffic arrives, almost nobody pays. Thin AI wrappers add fast novelty churn and thin margins on top of that.

### Cited Findings
- A September 2026 synthesis of 423 public founder posts on indie SaaS failure found the recurring pattern: product shipped, some traffic, almost nobody paid ("no buyers, not bad code") — [Collab365 Spaces](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/indie-saas-post-mortems-point-to-no-buyers-not-bad-code-utt3f3m2)
- "Solo SaaS launches still stall when proof and traffic come after the build" — [Collab365 Spaces](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/solo-saas-launches-still-stall-when-proof-and-traf-ICMbS6)

- AI apps churn 30% faster than non-AI apps while earning 41% more per user (RevenueCat via coverage) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- 84% of companies report at least 6% gross-margin erosion from AI infrastructure costs (2025 Mavvrik study; about companies generally, not solo founders) — [search result set; attribution to a specific page not confirmed](https://masterofcode.com/blog/why-ai-startups-fail/amp)
- A founding team publicly walked away from their AI SaaS, saying bootstrapping did not work (details not read) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/we-walked-away-from-our-ai-saas-here-s-why-bootstrapping-didn-t-work-70797121fa)

- Audience without offer fails too: 32K followers, 0 buyers — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/32k-followers-0-buyers-last-month-i-tried-again-people-pre-paid-before-launch-637829fc0c)
- One-and-done B2C payments (1.44 payments per payer) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/1k-mrr-1700-users-5-months-solo-and-the-math-just-told-me-to-pivot-to-b2b-3791c8e98c)

### Inferences
- Failure modes in order of frequency in this evidence: (1) built before any buyer was identified; (2) no repeatable channel; (3) B2C one-off usage with no reason to renew; (4) thin wrapper that the model vendor or a free tool replaces; (5) cost per active free user exceeding revenue. I found plenty of evidence for 1-4 and only general evidence for 5.
- For the reader, Groq's low inference prices reduce risk 5, but free-tier abuse on a consumer product can still exceed revenue; rate limits and paid-only heavy features are prudent. (No sourced Groq cost case study was found.)
- Feature sprawl was named in the brief but I found no specific sourced example.

## 7. Fast validation methods that work

### Takeaway
Money, not signups, is the valid signal: pre-orders, paid beta seats, or a manually delivered service. A small number of paying customers (3-5) before writing the product is the commonly cited bar; waitlists alone mislead.

### Cited Findings
- "The waitlist illusion": signups do not validate demand; sell pre-orders or paid beta spots instead — [Alex Cloudstar](https://www.alexcloudstar.com/blog/the-waitlist-illusion/)
- Founder with 32K followers and zero buyers succeeded on the next attempt by getting people to pre-pay before launch — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/32k-followers-0-buyers-last-month-i-tried-again-people-pre-paid-before-launch-637829fc0c)
- "Building a pre-order page has been the best decision of my indie hacking career" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-pre-order-page-has-been-the-best-decision-of-my-indie-hacking-career-effb6e0699)
- Heuristic thresholds (inconsistent, rough): 3-5 pre-ordering customers to validate; pre-order rate of 3%+ as a green light; landing-page signup rate of 10%+ as strong; one example got 4% signups from 200 visits — [Learning Loop](https://learningloop.io/blog/sell-before-you-build), [eChai](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-demand-validation/landing-page-waitlist-test-before-inventory), [Demand Curve](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-248) (which page carries which threshold is not confirmed)
- Concierge example: a founder manually analysed meeting recordings for his first 20 users before automating — [newsletter](https://thestarterpreneur.beehiiv.com/p/issue-47-stop-building-in-the-dark) (page attribution not confirmed)
- Reddit-derived claim, weak evidence: 44 of 47 SaaS founders who reached USD 10K MRR sold a manual service before writing code — [Discury](https://discury.io/pulse-why-saas-founders-fail-9748) (page attribution not confirmed)
- India: community first, product later. Delta Education had no product when it started, only a Telegram group; the test series came the following admission cycle — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/from-telegram-group-to-a-profitable-startup-student-turns-admission-queries-into-rs10-lakh-business-556058-2026-09-20)
- India: services before SaaS; pre-sell cohort produced about USD 3K before any platform revenue — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-for-india-s-non-metro-founders-3k-revenue-0-mrr-saas-here-s-what-s-actually-working-42f0fbff80)
- Meesho's founders handled WhatsApp sellers' transactions manually before building the platform — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/meesho-doing-things-that-dont-scale/)
- Free tool first, then charge: Mockey and Superpower ChatGPT (Section 1) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/hitting-12k-mrr-just-11-months-after-turning-a-free-side-project-into-an-ai-business-wUZgSLPMYgJMQkQ4b0ZB)

### Inferences
- A practical sequence consistent with the evidence: (1) pick a narrow buyer who already pays for something adjacent; (2) deliver the result by hand (WhatsApp/Telegram group, spreadsheet, or a done-for-you service using the LLM behind the scenes) and charge via plain UPI or a payment link; (3) stop if fewer than 3-5 people pay within two to three weeks; (4) only then build, and list the product in one marketplace or as a free tool.
- In India the WhatsApp/Telegram-service route doubles as distribution, since the group is both the validation and the first channel.
- Because the reader can build quickly, the temptation is to skip step 2; the post-mortem evidence in Section 6 says that is exactly the common failure.

### Gaps
- No sourced conversion benchmarks for pre-sales by solo founders; thresholds above are heuristics from blogs.
- No sourced Indian example of "sold a spreadsheet first" was found.
- No revenue figures for the concierge example.
