# Cross-market mechanics: proven product mechanisms abroad with weak or no Indian equivalent (as of November 2026)

Reader's notes on how to use this file:

- 23 mechanisms are catalogued (M1 to M23), grouped under the seven key questions.
- "Cited Findings" hold only what a source said in this session. Anything about how to adapt or build is in "Inferences" and is my reasoning, not a sourced fact.
- Most usage numbers are company claims or third-party tracker estimates. Each is labelled. Older data carries its year.
- The search tool used is US-only and returned mostly English sources. "No Indian equivalent found" means none surfaced in these searches, not that none exists. Korean, Japanese and Chinese-language sources were not searched.

## 1. East Asia study culture products (Korea, Japan, China): usage and mechanics with no Indian equivalent

### Takeaway
The East Asian mechanics with the largest proven usage and the weakest Indian equivalents are (a) the group study timer with live peer ranking (YPT, about 5 million users claimed), (b) the book-centric social study log (Studyplus, 9 million claimed), and (c) photo-checking of a whole handwritten worksheet aimed at parents (Xiaoyuan Kousuan, 70 million problems checked a day in 2019). Indian clones of the group timer exist but all look tiny, and Indian JEE/NEET aspirants already use the Korean app directly.

### Cited Findings

- A third-party tracker lists 5.6M Google Play installs, 62.5K ratings and about +18.9K installs a week (estimate, not audited) — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.pallo.passiontimerscoped)
- The App Store listing describes it as a study app "for 5 million people" (developer marketing claim) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yeolpumta/id1441909643)
- Features described: subject-based study timer, sharing study activity with friends in groups, to-do list tied to tracked time, blocking of other apps during focus sessions; user complaints include freezing, crashes and time-recording bugs — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/yeolpumta--ypt/about); [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/topics/yeolpumta)
- The app is listed on the Indian App Store as "YPT - Study Group", and a thread titled "study tracker app yeolpumta" exists on an Indian JEE/NEET aspirant subreddit (2024), showing Indian aspirants use the Korean product directly — [App Store India](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/ypt-yeolpumta/id1441909643); [Reddit mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/2026JEENEETards/comments/1do77z3/study_tracker_app_yeolpumta/labko7j/?context=3)

### Inferences

**M1. Group study timer with live "who is studying now" ranking (YPT, Korea)**
- Why it works: it turns invisible effort (hours) into a visible, comparable number inside a small group of real peers. The live state ("3 of your 8 groupmates are studying right now") is the hook, not the timer. It is social proof plus mild loss of face, with no content cost.
- India status: clones exist (Plexus Timer, Maarg, Study Room, Study Grid Prep) but none showed any scale, and Indian aspirants use YPT itself. So the gap is not "no product", it is "no localised winner".
- What must change: exam-shaped groups (JEE 2027 droppers, NEET repeaters, CA Inter, UPSC, board Class 12), Hinglish and regional-language UI, very low data use, works on 3 to 4 GB RAM Android phones, group join by WhatsApp link. YPT's reported bugs (timer loss, crashes) are a direct opening: reliability of the recorded time is the product.
- Solo build: Firestore document per group with member presence (Realtime Database presence is cheaper for this), a server-timestamped start/stop to prevent clock cheating, a daily and weekly group board. No LLM needed. Add to the existing app as a tab, not a new app.
- Law: study time per child is behavioural data. For under-18s it needs verifiable parental consent and may fall under the tracking bar (see section 7). Lower risk if groups are private and invite-only.

**M2. Book-centric social study log (Studyplus, Japan)**
- The difference from M1: the unit is the reference book, not the hour. Users log "32 pages of this specific book" and see everyone else using the same book, which creates a per-book community and a per-book review database. The second business is B2B: a dashboard sold to cram schools, plus ads to exam-takers.
- Why it works: Indian exam prep is also organised around a canon of books (HC Verma, NCERT, RD Sharma, MTG, Laxmikanth). Per-book progress and "how long did toppers take on this chapter" is information no Indian app surfaced in these searches.
- India status: no equivalent found.
- Solo build: seed a catalogue of 200 to 300 standard books with chapter lists (the LLM can draft chapter lists, a human must verify), let users log chapters done, show aggregate pace per book. The B2B angle (a dashboard for small tuition centres) is the realistic revenue path.

**M3. Whole-worksheet photo checking for parents, plus timed arithmetic duels (Xiaoyuan Kousuan, China)**
- Why it works: the buyer is the parent, and the job is "check 40 sums in 5 seconds", not "teach my child". It removes a daily chore, so usage is daily. The duel mode gives the child a reason to open it.
- India status: no equivalent surfaced for primary-class handwritten worksheet checking. Photo doubt-solving for a single question is widely available in India; whole-page marking for Classes 1 to 5 was not found.
- What must change: Indian notebook handwriting, Devanagari and regional numerals in some states, poor lighting and low-end cameras. Positioning for mothers and for tuition teachers who mark 30 notebooks a night may be stronger than for students.
- Solo build: a vision-capable model through an API reads a photo and returns per-sum right/wrong with boxes. Arithmetic is verifiable in code, so the model only has to transcribe; the app computes correctness itself. That cuts hallucination risk. Accuracy on messy handwriting is the main unknown and must be tested before building anything else.

**M4. Print-on-demand practice sheets from the child's own mistakes (Xiaoyuan machine, China)**
- The proven part is the loop scan mistakes, generate a personalised set, print on paper. China sells this as hardware; the same loop works as a PDF.
- India status: no equivalent found. Indian parents and schools distrust screen time, and print shops are everywhere, so a PDF is a natural fit.
- Solo build: generate a one-page A4 PDF of 10 questions from the app's existing error data, with a QR code that opens the answers and lets the parent photograph the completed sheet (links to M3). This is a few days of work on the existing stack.

**M5. Always-on "study with me" rooms (Bilibili, China)**
- Why it works: passive company and supervision, at zero content cost to the platform.
- India status: Indian "study with me" streams exist on YouTube (not measured in this session). A room inside a study app tied to the timer (M1) was not found at scale.
- Solo build caution: live video is expensive and is a moderation risk with minors. A camera-free version (avatars at desks, live count, shared 25/5 timer) captures most of the effect at near-zero cost.

**M6. Hardware and study cafes**: not solo-buildable. The transferable piece of the tablet boom is M4. The transferable piece of study cafes is the unmanned booking and access software, which is a B2B product for Indian reading rooms and libraries (a large informal sector in exam towns); no data on that market was gathered here.

### Gaps
- Mildang (Korea): only its existence and positioning (1:1 tutoring with AI content for middle and high school) surfaced; no revenue or student numbers, and nothing on its camera-on-desk supervision method — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/ihateflyingbugs/)
- Atama+, Studysapuri (Japan), the Timing app (China): no usable results. No figures are given here.
- Korean study cafe total store count and market size: not found.
- Current (2025 to 2026) usage of Xiaoyuan Kousuan: only 2019 claims found.
- YPT's own official user count and revenue: not found; only the store-listing claim.
- Download counts for Indian group-timer apps: not found, so their small scale is an inference from their absence in coverage, not a measured fact.

## 2. Southeast Asia, Latin America and global homework helpers: what worked in price-sensitive mobile-first markets

### Takeaway
The pattern that worked is a free, high-frequency utility (photo doubt-solving or peer Q&A) used as an organic funnel into paid live group classes. India already has scale in the free layer (Brainly had 25 million Indian users by 2020), so the arbitrage here is weak; the remaining gap is the human-in-the-loop paid micro-answer supply side.

### Cited Findings
- CoLearn (Indonesia): its AI assistant "Tanya" was receiving more than 10 million questions a month, and 90% of new students arrived organically; it combines doubt solving with live tutoring classes and raised a $27M Series A — [YesPress profile](https://yespress.io/colearn); [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/sea/story/the-interview-abhay-saboo-does-quick-math-for-colearns-edtech-gains/)
- CoLearn's own survey claims 80% of students improved their grades (self-reported, not independent) — [YesPress profile](https://yespress.io/colearn)
- Ruangguru (Indonesia) raised a $55M Series C extension led by Tiger Global and GGV (2021) and expanded into professional skills with Skill Academy in 2019 — [CompassList, 2021](https://www.compasslist.com/insights/indonesian-edtechs-attract-funding-even-as-students-head-back-to-school)
- Descomplica (Brazil): reached 30 million students, with courses live-streamed seven days a week; 239,000 annual subscribers and revenue of R$55 million in 2019 — [World Economic Forum profile](https://weforum.org/organizations/descomplica)
- Brainly: more than 15 million active users in India within two years (2019), more than 25 million in India (2020), 250 million globally (2020) — [YourStory, 2019](https://yourstory.com/2019/03/polish-edtech-startup-brainly-ykhsxy9ni6); [YourStory, 2020](https://yourstory.com/2020/10/app-fridays-students-free-app-homework-brainly-edtech)
- In late 2025 India was the second-largest audience for brainly.com after the US, and brainly.in is a separate tracked domain — [Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/brainly.com/overview)
- Gauth: monthly active users rose to over 1.2 million by March 2025 and its website had about 4.7 million visits that month (Sensor Tower; the report's geography is its Brazil series, so this is not a global figure) — [Sensor Tower, Q1 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q1-br-leading-3-Education---Training-brands)

### Inferences

**M7. Free doubt-solving as an organic funnel to cheap live group classes (CoLearn, Indonesia)**
- Why it works economically: the doubt tool gives daily intent data (which chapter, which child is stuck) at near-zero acquisition cost; classes monetise it. Descomplica's numbers (239,000 subscribers on R$55M) imply roughly R$230 per subscriber per year, a low-price, high-volume model.
- India status: saturated by funded players. Not recommended as a solo project, but the funnel logic applies to any feature below: the free utility should produce the data that sells the paid thing.

**M8. Peer Q&A with points (Brainly)**
- India status: Brainly itself is large in India. No gap. Included so the report writer can rule it out.

**M9. Paid human micro-answers by vetted students (QANDA Teacher, Korea)**
- The mechanism: a queue of photographed questions, answered by vetted university students who are paid per accepted answer. It exists as a separate earner app, which is itself a product for the job-seeker and college-student segment.
- India status: no direct equivalent surfaced in this session (paid micro-answer queue with a dedicated earner app). I could not confirm absence; several Indian doubt apps historically used hired tutors.
- Why it could still matter in 2026: LLMs answer most questions free, so the human is paid for the cases the AI fails or the student distrusts (diagrams, long derivations, "explain like my teacher would"). The college-student earner side is the unusual part: a micro-income product for young people.
- Solo build: Firestore queue, answer acceptance by the asker, UPI payouts through a payment aggregator's payout product. Risks: KYC and tax on payouts, quality control, payments to anyone under 18 (avoid; require 18+ earners).

### Gaps
- Zenius, Pahamify, Me Salva, Photomath: no usable figures surfaced. Nothing is stated about them.
- Pricing tactics of Ruangguru and CoLearn (price points, instalments): not found.
- Current Indian user numbers for Brainly, Gauth and Photomath: not found; the India figures above are from 2019 and 2020.

## 3. Social and accountability mechanics from outside education

### Takeaway
The strongest transferable mechanisms with evidence are scheduled one-to-one body doubling (Focusmate, 12 million+ sessions), commitment contracts with a human referee (stickK data on 75,828 goals), and the annual shareable recap (Spotify Wrapped reached 200 million users in 24 hours in 2025). Teen-virality mechanics (Gas, NGL) worked for weeks and ended in shutdown or regulatory bans, and money-staked mechanics now carry real legal risk in India.

### Cited Findings

**Body doubling and co-working**
- Focusmate states 12 million+ completed sessions and 500 million+ minutes of focus across 150+ countries (company site) — [Focusmate](https://focusmate.com/)
- Pricing: free tier of three 50-minute sessions a week; Plus at $8/month billed annually or $12 monthly — [Lifestack review](https://lifestack.ai/blog/focusmate-reviews)
- The founder describes reaching seven-figure annual recurring revenue by building community into the product — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-a-7-figure-arr-by-building-a-community-into-his-product-6LeFoYyWrOyqlw9aLqUf)
- The Study Together Discord server lists 1,059,812 members and calls itself the largest study and productivity server on Discord — [Discord](https://discord.com/servers/study-together-595999872222756885)
- StudyStream is described as an online study room platform with 1 million members from 215+ countries; its Discord has roughly 194K to 200K members — [Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/studystream/); [Discord Bot List](https://discordbotlist.com/servers/studystream)

**Commitment contracts**
- stickK's own figures: 87.1% success with a punishment stake, 82.8% with positive reinforcement only, 42.7% with no stakes (self-reported, subject to self-selection) — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/technology-innovation/to-make-better-decisions-consider-punishing-yourself/)
- Academic analysis of 75,828 public stickK goals (Nov 2013 to Feb 2018): success depends heavily on how financial and social commitment devices are configured, and a mixed setting is beneficial — [KAIST paper, 2019](https://ic.kaist.ac.kr/files/papers/lee2019commitment.pdf)
- 29% of stickK commitments had monetary stakes and 19% had external accountability (a referee); both significantly raise success, and stakes pledged to an anti-charity outperform stakes to a charity — [ICWSM paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/download/31440/33600)
- In the randomised smoking-cessation commitment-contract trial, the intent-to-treat quit rate was about 10%, versus about 52% among those who actually took up the contract, which shows how much self-selection inflates headline success rates — [Beeminder blog summary](https://blog.beeminder.com/smoking)

**Teen virality: Gas and NGL**
- Gas (anonymous compliment polls among schoolmates) reached about 10 million downloads and roughly $5 to $7 million in consumer spending within months of its 2022 launch; Discord acquired it in January 2023 — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/17/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-media-app-for-teens); [TechBriefly](https://techbriefly.com/2023/01/18/discord-acquires-gas)
- Gas was hit by a false sex-trafficking rumour that damaged downloads; Discord shut it down about nine months after buying it, when monthly downloads had fallen to about 2,900 — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/17/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-media-app-for-teens); [dot.LA](https://dot.la/tag/gas)
- An expert critique at the time said the app "monetizes [teens'] anxiety" by charging to reveal hints about who voted — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6718229)
- NGL: in July 2024 the US FTC banned it from serving users under 18, the first such order against a digital platform, with a $5 million settlement. Allegations: it sent fake computer-generated messages to drive engagement and falsely claimed AI moderation prevented cyberbullying — [TechCrunch, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/ftc-bans-ngl-from-offering-its-anonymous-social-app-to-minors); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ftc-bans-anonymous-messaging-app-ngl-for-minors-rcna160897)

**Single daily moment: BeReal**
- Voodoo acquired BeReal for EUR 500 million in June 2024; Voodoo cited 40 million active users, half using it at least six days a week; outside estimates put daily users near 25 million — [TechCrunch, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/15/deal-dive-bereal-got-its-best-case-scenario-exit)
- BeReal's daily users fell 61% between October 2022 and March 2023 (Sensor Tower via NYT) and peak was around 70 million — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/bereal-sold-to-french-video-game-developer-for-500-million/718675/)

**Recap and activity feed**
- Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached 200 million users within 24 hours (62 hours the year before), with engagement up 19%; a "Wrapped Party" feature lets friends compare — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91454685/spotify-wrapped-2025-eagerly-checking-your-listening-age-everyone-else-is-too)
- Strava reports over 180 million users in more than 185 countries (company trend report) — [Strava press release via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/13961203642123264)

**Duolingo social mechanics**
- Duolingo passed 50 million daily active users in 2025 and guided to about 20% DAU growth in 2026 — [MarketBeat, Q4 2025 call](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/duolingo-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-02-26/)
- Duolingo added a universal matching system so learners without friends on the app can still join Friends Quests — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-highlights/)
- No controlled figure isolating the retention effect of leagues or Friends Quests was found; third-party analyses assert it without numbers — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/4/14/duolingo-how-the-15b-app-uses-gaming-principles-to-supercharge-dau-growth)

### Inferences

**M10. Scheduled one-to-one body doubling (Focusmate, US)**
- Why it works: a booked appointment with a specific person creates a small social cost for not showing up; stating the goal aloud at the start is a pre-commitment. Focusmate's free-three-sessions tier shows the paywall is on frequency.
- India status: no Indian equivalent found for students. Discord study servers are global and English-first.
- What must change: video between strangers is unacceptable for minors and for many Indian families. Use same-gender or same-exam matching, audio-off, camera optional or replaced by a periodic desk photo; 18+ for stranger matching, friends-only for under-18s. Late-night slots (10 pm to 2 am) matter for Indian aspirants. Price in the Rs 49 to 99 a month range rather than $8.
- Solo build: slot booking in Firestore, pairing by exam and slot, a shared timer and text check-in at start and end. Skip video in version one; a WebRTC provider can come later.

**M11. Commitment contract with a human referee, without money (stickK)**
- The evidence says the referee and social component matter and that mixed devices work best; the headline 87% is inflated by selection. That is useful: the referee can be kept and the money dropped.
- India-specific twist: the referee is a parent, elder sibling or tutor on WhatsApp, which fits the existing parent-update feature. The "stake" can be non-monetary and pre-agreed inside the family (phone time, a cricket match), recorded in the app but not handled by it.
- India status: no equivalent found.
- Law: a cash stake is risky. See section 7; a deposit that is forfeited or returned based on an outcome may be argued to fall near the broad "other stakes" language of the 2025 online gaming law, and minors cannot be party to such an arrangement. Do not build the cash version without legal advice.

**M12. One random daily moment (BeReal) applied to study**
- Adaptation: once a day at a random time during the user's declared study window, the group gets two minutes to post a photo of the page in front of them. It is proof of work that cannot be staged in advance, and it is cheaper than a timer.
- Caution from the data: BeReal's use fell 61% in five months. Novelty decays, so this should be a feature inside a group (M1), not a product.
- Law: photos from minors are personal data; keep it to private groups and delete after 24 hours.

**M13. Annual and exam-cycle recap (Spotify Wrapped)**
- Adaptation: "Your JEE prep, wrapped": hours, chapters, strongest topic, a "study age" or archetype, shareable as an image to WhatsApp status and Instagram stories. Timing matters: release on the day after the exam, or at term end, when students are already posting.
- India status: no study-app equivalent found. Cheap to build from data the app already has; the LLM writes the archetype copy.

**M14. Segments: leaderboards on a small fixed piece of the route (Strava)**
- Adaptation: instead of one global leaderboard, a leaderboard per chapter or per standard problem set ("fastest accurate run through 20 questions on rotational motion"), with a personal-record history. Being top of one small segment is achievable for an average student in a way a global rank is not, and it localises (school, city, coaching batch).
- India status: not found in this form. I could not confirm absence inside the large test-series apps.

**M15. Cooperative quests with auto-matching for users who have no friends on the app (Duolingo)**
- The specific, copyable detail is the matching: a solo user is paired with a stranger for a shared weekly target, which makes a social feature work at low user counts. This matters for a solo developer with a small user base.
- Evidence strength: weak. Duolingo shipped it and kept it, but no isolated effect size is public.

**M16. Anonymous positive polls among classmates (Gas): do not build as-is**
- It proved explosive growth through school-by-school density, and it also proved fragility: a rumour collapsed it, and the paid "who voted for you" hint was criticised as monetising anxiety. NGL's version ended in a regulator banning minors.
- If used at all: non-anonymous, academic-only recognitions ("who explained a doubt best this week") inside a class group, with no paid reveal and no fake messages. Under Indian law, an app whose core audience is minors and whose loop depends on profiling peers is the highest-risk category in this file.

### Gaps
- Flow Club, Forfeit, Beeminder user numbers, Locket Widget downloads, Strava segment-specific usage, Duolingo league effect size: nothing usable was found. No numbers are given.
- No peer-reviewed study of Focusmate's effect on task completion surfaced.
- Whether Indian students use global Discord study servers in large numbers: not measured.

## 4. Peer-to-peer and marketplace mechanics

### Takeaway
The notes marketplace is proven abroad (Stuvia has paid out over EUR 25 million to students) and was tried in India a decade ago (Notesgen) without a visible winner today, which makes it the clearest "real gap but with a reason" in this file. Senior-to-junior mentoring is not a gap: PhysicsWallah's Disha and several startups already sell IIT/AIIMS mentor access, and Topmate covers generic paid calls.

### Cited Findings
- Stuvia: 50,000+ documents sold every month, over EUR 25 million paid out to students since 2010, weekly payouts, top sellers earning over $2,500 in an academic year (company claims) — [Stuvia](https://www.stuvia.com/en-us/sell-study-notes)
- Stuvia's commission is reported as 30% by a third-party review (not confirmed against Stuvia's terms) — [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/stuvia/)
- Studocu raised $50 million as it passed 15 million users (2021) — [TechCrunch, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2149643)
- A sponsored 2026 article cites 60 million monthly users, 50 million documents and 120,000 schools for Studocu (sponsored content, treat with caution) — [pulse.bot sponsored piece](https://www.pulse.bot/saas/news/how-studocu-is-redefining-exam-prep-with-ai-and-over-50-million-documents-sponsored-3edfa976-05ab-4b4e-8792-b126fedc7f1b/)
- Studocu's model: upload your own documents to unlock access, or pay a monthly premium instead — [Idea Usher](https://ideausher.com/blog/launch-platform-studocu/)
- India, Notesgen: a Delhi-based peer-to-peer notes marketplace for typed or handwritten notes across 14 categories including UPSC; uploaders received 60% of sale price; it crossed 400K users and raised seed funding (coverage dated 2015 to 2018; current status unknown) — [YourStory, 2015](https://yourstory.com/2015/07/notesgen); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/notesgen-raises-seed-funding)
- India, ToppersNotes: a Jaipur company selling its own printed notes for IAS, GATE, SSC, NEET, JEE and state exams; turned profitable within a year of its first institutional round (2022) and raised $1 million. It is a publisher, not a peer marketplace — [YourStory, 2022](https://yourstory.com/2022/10/toppersnotes-turns-profitable-within-year-of-raising-funding/amp); [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/test-prep-startup-toppersnotes-raises-1m-in-funding-led-by-inflection-point-ventures)
- India, creator storefronts publish guides on selling handwritten notes as scanned PDFs, indicating individual sellers already do this off-marketplace — [SuperProfile](https://superprofile.bio/guides/sell-notes-online-india)
- India, mentoring: PhysicsWallah's Disha assigns each student a personal mentor who is a top ranker from AIIMS or IIT, reachable by chat, audio or video; PW also runs a "Bhaiya-Didi" mentorship programme — [Disha](https://disha.pw.live); [PW news, 2026](https://www.pw.live/news/bhaiya-didi-mentorship-jee-neet-vishwas-diwas-2026)
- Other Indian mentoring players: MentorAstra claims 20,000+ students and 100+ mentors; eSaral claims 1,550+ IITians and 2,000+ doctors mentored; Exprto connects students with recent JEE/NEET toppers (all self-reported) — [MentorAstra](https://iitbombaymentors.vercel.app/about); [eSaral](https://www.esaral.com/mentorship/); [Exprto](https://yourstory.com/companies/exprto)
- Topmate (India) publishes price and booking-time data from paid bookings (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, page updated 1 Nov 2026); a Topmate executive claims some part-time creators earn Rs 4 lakh or more a month (executive claim, unaudited) — [Topmate reports](https://topmate.io/reports); [Hawky podcast](https://hawky.ai/podcast/anuj-niranjan-topmate)

### Inferences

**M17. Peer notes marketplace with upload-to-unlock (Studocu) and paid listings (Stuvia)**
- Why it works: notes are produced anyway, so supply is free; the buyer wants the exact course at the exact institution. Upload-to-unlock solves cold start, because every taker becomes a giver.
- India gap: real but explained. Indian notes move free on Telegram and WhatsApp, so willingness to pay for a PDF is low, and piracy of coaching material is rampant. A marketplace must sell what Telegram cannot: verification (this is the real rank-holder's notebook), structure (indexed by chapter and searchable through OCR) and currency (this year's syllabus).
- The narrower, more defensible version: verified topper answer sheets with the marks awarded, for board exams and UPSC Mains, where the value is seeing how marks were given. I found no evidence either way on whether this is sold commercially in India.
- Risks: copyright in coaching-institute material, ownership of evaluated answer scripts (board and institute rules), sellers under 18 (payments to minors must go through a guardian), and takedown workload.
- Solo build: Firebase Storage, OCR plus LLM tagging to chapter level, upload-to-unlock credits first (no payments, so no payout compliance), paid listings later.

**M18. Verified senior micro-calls (15 minutes)**
- Not a gap for JEE/NEET: Disha, eSaral, Exprto, MentorAstra and Topmate cover it. The open niches are below the national-exam tier: state engineering and medical counselling choices, specific-college seniors ("should I take this branch at this college"), diploma-to-degree lateral entry, first-job seekers talking to someone one year into the same role, and regional-language mentors. These are low-ticket, high-volume and too small for funded players.
- Solo build: verification by college ID plus an institutional email check, Rs 99 to 199 for 15 minutes, payment through an aggregator, calls over an ordinary phone bridge or a meeting link. Mentors must be 18+.

### Gaps
- Course Hero: no data gathered.
- Notesgen's current status, and why it did not scale: not found. The Telegram-piracy explanation above is my inference.
- Whether topper answer-sheet sales exist commercially in India, and their legal status: not found.
- Topmate's total user count and take rate: not found in this session.
- Mentza: no results.

## 5. Hardware-light and offline mechanics (paper, QR, WhatsApp, SMS, IVR)

### Takeaway
WhatsApp and SMS delivery has huge proven reach in India and Africa (ConveGenius reached 14.5 million students on WhatsApp in a year; Eneza passed 6 million learners by SMS), but the evidence on learning outcomes is mixed: one registered Rocket Learning trial found the base WhatsApp-group intervention engaged parents and did not improve math or language scores. Reach is proven; effect is not automatic.

### Cited Findings
- ConveGenius (India): WhatsApp integration took it to 14.5 million students in a year, with 20 subject curricula in 10 languages — [Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/whatsapp-becomes-the-new-classroom-leaving-edtech-companies-behind/)
- After schools reopened, WhatsApp use for learning dropped and ConveGenius moved to its own app ecosystem, SwiftChat, now in about 40% of the country's schools with about 1.4 crore (14 million) monthly active users — [NITI Aayog Frontier Tech](https://frontiertech.niti.gov.in/story/this-digital-platform-is-using-ai-for-skill-development-in-indian-schools/)
- A different source cites a claim of over 100 million students for SwiftChat; the two figures are not reconciled — [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/convegenius-builds-swiftchat-conversational-ai-platform-on-aws-1380450)
- Rocket Learning (India): reached over 4 million children and families across 140 districts through WhatsApp parent groups run with government anganwadis — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/digital-parent-learning-communities/)
- A registered trial of technology-delivered preschool services in India reports that the base intervention (structured activities through WhatsApp groups) was implemented at scale and engaged parents but did not improve children's math and language outcomes; the more intensive version cost about $3.39 (Rs 285) per student — [AEA RCT Registry 17446](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17446)
- A third-party RCT cited by a funder found a 0.2 standard deviation gain in developmental milestones for ages 3 to 5, with no effect on executive function; the funder notes open questions about interpreting the result — [Mulago Foundation](https://www.mulagofoundation.org/portfolio/rocket-learning)
- Eneza Education (Kenya, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire): over 6 million learners reached by SMS for Grades 4 to 12 as of September 2020 (secondary source, 2020) — [Engineering for Change](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/?p=39584)
- A GSMA-commissioned formative evaluation of Eneza in Côte d'Ivoire (Oct 2019 to Feb 2020) found engagement depended on parent awareness and partnerships, and recommended analysing lesson completion and quiz pass rates by subject — [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/blog/five-key-insights-from-evaluating-a-gsma-edtech-grantee-in-cote-divoire/)
- M-Shule (Kenya): a peer-reviewed analysis found SMS-delivered literacy material had a positive and relatively cost-effective impact on learning outcomes; its direct reach is small, about 23,000 learners — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/375827); [SEWF](https://sewfonline.com/mshule/)

### Inferences

**M19. WhatsApp-first delivery to the parent, with a paper task (Rocket Learning pattern)**
- Why reach is cheap: no install, no login, works on the parent's phone, which is the phone the child actually has access to.
- The lesson from the evidence: messages alone engage and may not teach. The versions that showed gains were more intensive. So the WhatsApp channel should carry a concrete task and a return loop (photo of completed work, a two-question check), not content alone.
- Also note the ConveGenius lesson: WhatsApp use fell once schools reopened and the company moved users into its own app. WhatsApp is an acquisition and nudge channel, not a home.
- India status: exists at scale, but through government and NGO channels for early and primary grades. A paid consumer version for Classes 6 to 10 parents was not found in these searches.
- Solo build: WhatsApp Business API through a provider, a daily one-task message, a reply-with-photo loop feeding M3. Per-conversation charges apply; cost per user must be modelled before launch.

**M20. SMS or missed-call quizzes for feature phones (Eneza, M-Shule)**
- India relevance is narrower than in Kenya because smartphone and cheap data penetration are higher, but it fits two cases: students whose phones are taken away in the exam year, and households with one shared smartphone. A missed-call-to-get-a-callback quiz costs the user nothing.
- Solo build: an Indian cloud telephony provider with IVR and missed-call numbers; DLT registration is required for commercial SMS in India (regulatory requirement stated from background knowledge; verify current TRAI rules before building).

**M21. QR-linked paper (from M4)**
- A printed sheet or sticker in the child's own textbook with a QR code that opens the explanation, quiz or parent-check for that exact page. No Indian consumer product doing this for the child's existing books surfaced; publishers and DIKSHA do print QR codes in textbooks (stated from background knowledge, not checked in this session).

### Gaps
- No data was found on missed-call learning products or their scale.
- Results of the 2025 Haryana Rocket Learning trial (0 to 3 years) are not yet available in the registry entry — [AEA RCT Registry 16756](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/16756)
- Cost per WhatsApp conversation in India in 2026 and DLT rules were not researched.
- Which Rocket Learning trial produced the 0.2 SD result is not stated in the source.

## 6. Mechanisms from games and creator tools

### Takeaway
User-generated question sets wrapped in swappable game modes (Blooket, Kahoot) are proven at very large scale and are underused by Indian tutors, which is the opening. Appointment-based live trivia with cash prizes peaked fast and died in both the US (HQ, 2.3 million concurrent in February 2018, shut 2020) and India (Loco and BrainBaazi, about 500,000 concurrent each in 2018), and cash prizes are now legally hazardous in India.

### Cited Findings
- Blooket says there are more than 20 million sets made by users (company claim relayed by a competitor blog); another blog cites over 10 million students and over 2 million sets, so figures conflict — [AhaSlides review](https://ahaslides.com/et/blog/blooket-review/); [TriviaMaker](https://triviamaker.com/blooket-vs-kahoot/)
- HQ Trivia peaked at more than 2.3 million concurrent players in February 2018, declined steadily afterwards, and shut down in February 2020 after investors withdrew and an acquisition fell through — [TechCrunch, 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/14/hq-trivia-shuts-down)
- Reasons cited for HQ's decline: prize money split among too many winners, failure to evolve beyond guest hosts, a $20 minimum withdrawal for most of its life, stream lag in early rounds, and leadership turmoil — [TechCrunch, 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/14/trivia-game-of-thrones); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1370704/hq-trivia-most-disastrous-moments/)
- India, 2018: Loco had five million downloads with concurrent users peaking at half a million; BrainBaazi (Times Internet) also reported over half a million concurrent users — [YourStory, 2018](https://yourstory.com/2018/06/5-live-trivia-apps-indias-internet-universe-hooked)
- Loco moved to game streaming in 2020; later reporting said it lacked a robust monetisation model and spent heavily on streamers and tournament rights without returns — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/gaming-news/locos-avod-model-fails-will-it-bounce-back-37278.htm)
- After the August 2025 law, Dream11 said it lost about 95% of revenue and pivoted to "second-screen" sports entertainment and free fantasy games; MPL moved to free-to-play; Probo discontinued real-money operations — [YourStory, 2025](https://yourstory.com/2025/08/dream11-loses-95-revenue-gaming-ban-wont-cut-jobs-winzo); [TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/21/as-india-bans-real-money-games-dream-sports-mpl-start-pulling-the-plug); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/dream11-pivots-to-second-screen-sports-entertainment-after-real-money-gaming-ban)

### Inferences

**M22. Tutor-made question sets played through interchangeable game modes (Blooket, Gimkit, Kahoot)**
- Why it works: the teacher supplies content and distribution (a class of 30 joins by code); the platform supplies the game wrapper. Each set is reusable by other teachers, so content compounds. The same set played in different modes stays fresh.
- India status: Quizizz (founded in India, now global) and Kahoot are available, but they are built for classrooms with a projector and one device per child. The Indian tuition teacher with a WhatsApp group of 40 students and no projector is not served: no equivalent built for that was found.
- Adaptation: asynchronous "join by WhatsApp link, play on your own phone tonight, results posted to the group tomorrow". The LLM turns a photo of the tutor's handwritten question paper into a set. The tutor is the customer and the channel; each tutor brings 30 to 200 students.
- Solo build: straightforward on the existing stack; one or two game modes are enough to test.

**M23. Scheduled live quiz and fantasy or prediction formats: avoid cash, and probably avoid the format**
- HQ, Loco and BrainBaazi show the same curve: a cash-prize novelty spike, then decay once prizes are split thin and the format stops changing. Costs (prizes, live production) scale with users while revenue does not.
- Fantasy-style "pick your team of toppers" or exam-result prediction markets: with money they are squarely what the 2025 Act bans. Without money they are legal in principle but ethically awkward when the "players" being predicted are minors' exam results. A self-prediction variant (predict your own mock-test score, scored for calibration) keeps the engaging part and is harmless; it also teaches self-assessment. No product doing this was found, and no usage evidence exists for it, so it is speculative.

### Gaps
- Kahoot, Gimkit and Quizizz user numbers: only unreliable blog figures surfaced; none are reported here.
- Collectible-card and speedrunning mechanics in learning products: not researched for lack of budget; no evidence gathered.
- Whether BrainBaazi was formally shut down, and when: not found.
- Indian tuition-teacher use of Quizizz or Kahoot: not measured.

## 7. Indian law and risk in 2026 for the strong candidates

### Takeaway
Two regimes shape almost every idea above: the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 (rules in force from 1 May 2026) bans any game where money is deposited expecting a monetary return, regardless of skill, and the DPDP Act with its 2025 Rules requires verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18 and bars tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children. Anything with cash stakes or cash prizes should be dropped; anything social for under-18s needs a parent-consent flow and private-by-default design.

### Cited Findings

**Online gaming law**
- The Act was notified on 22 August 2025; the Rules were notified by MeitY on 22 April 2026 and came into force on 1 May 2026 — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/social-media/1785870/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025-and-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-rules-2026); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/govt-notifies-online-gaming-act-officially-bans-all-real-money-games-154048.html)
- The skill-versus-chance distinction is largely sidelined: if money is deposited with an expectation of monetary return, the game is an online money game, and "other stakes" is defined broadly — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/indias-online-gaming-reset-decoding-proga-and-the-2026-rules/)
- In classifying a game, the regulator weighs the revenue model and whether rewards or in-game assets can be transferred, redeemed or monetised; the ban applies whether or not a game appears on the authority's published list — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/indias-online-gaming-reset-decoding-proga-and-the-2026-rules/)
- The framework has three tiers: online social games, e-sports and online money games; e-sports are excluded from the money-game definition — [Digital Terminal](https://digitalterminal.in/gaming/government-unveils-new-online-gaming-framework-2026-to-protect-users-and-promote-e-sports); [PIB document, April 2026](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/apr/doc2026430859401.pdf)
- Market effect: Dream11, MPL, Zupee, PokerBaazi and Probo halted real-money games in August 2025 — [TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/21/as-india-bans-real-money-games-dream-sports-mpl-start-pulling-the-plug)

**Children's data (DPDP)**
- The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025, with a phased timeline; one source describes the children's obligations taking effect after an 18-month phase-in — [Biometric Update, Nov 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/india-notifies-its-sweeping-digital-personal-data-protection-rules)
- Platforms must obtain verifiable parental consent before processing data of anyone under 18; a checkbox is not enough, and methods include digital locker tokens, document verification or consent managers — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-design-a-verifiable-parental-consent-process-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited, and parental consent does not lift this — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-children-s-data-are-you-compliant)
- The Fourth Schedule gives a limited exemption to educational institutions for tracking or behavioural monitoring, restricted to educational activities and the safety of enrolled children; exemptions also cover healthcare providers and crèches — [DPDP Rules, Rule 12 text](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule12.html); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-children-s-data-are-you-compliant)
- Sources disagree on rule numbering between the January 2025 draft and the notified version — [Bar and Bench on the draft](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/childs-personal-data-and-privacy-analysing-the-draft-dpdp-rules-2025)

**Foreign precedent on teen social products**
- The FTC's NGL order (July 2024) is the first to ban a platform from serving minors, over fake engagement messages and false moderation claims — [TechCrunch, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/ftc-bans-ngl-from-offering-its-anonymous-social-app-to-minors)

### Inferences

Risk rating per mechanism (my assessment; not legal advice, and a lawyer should confirm anything marked medium or high):

| Mechanism | Gaming-law risk | Children's-data risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 group timer, M2 study log | None | Medium | Study-time logs are behavioural data. Whether an app (not a school) can rely on the educational exemption is unclear; assume it cannot. Parent consent, private groups, no ads. |
| M3 photo checking, M4 print sheets, M21 QR paper | None | Low | Parent is the user. Photos of a child's work are still the child's data; delete after processing. |
| M5 rooms, M10 body doubling | None | High if video with strangers | Make stranger matching 18+ only. |
| M9 paid micro-answers, M17 notes market, M18 senior calls | None | Low if earners are 18+ | Payout KYC, tax, copyright takedowns. No payouts to minors. |
| M11 commitment contract | High with cash, none without | Medium | A cash deposit returned on success sits close to the broad "other stakes" wording. Build the referee-only version. |
| M12 daily photo, M13 recap, M14 segments, M15 quests | None | Medium | Public leaderboards of named minors should be avoided; use nicknames and private or school-level scope. |
| M16 anonymous polls | None | Very high | Proven harm pattern abroad; do not build for minors. |
| M19 WhatsApp to parents, M20 SMS/IVR | None | Low | Parent is the data principal for the channel. Telecom template and consent rules apply. |
| M22 tutor quiz sets | None if no cash prizes | Medium | Tutor acts for the class; still needs parent consent for under-18 accounts. |
| M23 cash trivia, fantasy, prediction markets | Prohibited with money | n/a | Free-to-play, non-redeemable points only. Self-prediction variant is safe. |

Other points:
- Rewards that can be redeemed or transferred are an explicit factor in classification, so in-app coins must never convert to cash, vouchers or anything tradable if any game-like mechanic is attached.
- A design that makes the parent the account holder and consent-giver (which the existing parent-update feature already points toward) lowers risk for nearly every mechanism in this file.
- From background knowledge, not verified this session: minors cannot enter binding contracts under Indian contract law, and UPI accounts for minors are restricted; any product that pays or charges a minor directly should route through a guardian.

Ranking for a solo developer, combining proof, India gap, build cost and legal risk (my judgement):
1. M22 tutor-made sets over WhatsApp links (the tutor is the distribution channel; clear gap; low risk).
2. M3 + M4 parent photo-check and printable mistake sheets (daily parent utility; proven at 70 million checks a day in China; no Indian equivalent found; technical risk is handwriting accuracy).
3. M1 + M13 group timer with exam-cycle recap (proven demand, Indians already use the Korean app; competition is weak but present).
4. M11 referee-only commitment contract tied to parent updates (cheap, evidence-backed, fits existing features).
5. M17 verified notes and answer-sheet exchange using upload-to-unlock (real gap, heavier operations and copyright load).
6. M10 scheduled study partner for 18+ aspirants (proven abroad, no Indian equivalent found, needs liquidity to work).

### Gaps
- The statutory text of the Act and both sets of Rules was not read directly; all points come from law-firm commentary and press. Rule numbers and the exact commencement date of the children's-data obligations should be checked against the Gazette.
- Whether a refundable commitment deposit counts as an "online money game" has no authority either way that I found.
- Whether a private study app qualifies as an "educational institution" for the Fourth Schedule exemption: not found.
- RBI and NPCI rules on payments to and from minors, TRAI DLT rules for SMS, and WhatsApp Business pricing in 2026: not researched.
- Tax treatment (GST, TDS) of marketplace payouts to student sellers: not researched.
