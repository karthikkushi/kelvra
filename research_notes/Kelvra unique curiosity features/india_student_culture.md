# Indian student exam culture: what students and parents search, share and need (curiosity hooks for Kelvra)

*Research notes compiled 5 Nov 2026. Scope: India, 2022-2026 (older data is marked). Method: web search plus press and official sources. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session for asercentre.org, theweek.in and deccanherald.com, so ASER figures and two other items come from secondary coverage or search snippets (flagged inline). Vendor and company claims are labelled. All "my calc" figures are my own arithmetic on the cited numbers.*

## 1. What Indian students search, share and obsess over around exams

### Takeaway
Exam talk in India circles a few high-anxiety numbers (raw marks, then percentile, then rank, then college seat) and "topper" artefacts (topper answer sheets, unofficial toppers lists, "500/500" stories). It also runs on rags-to-results self-study stories and social "study with me" formats. Most of it lives on YouTube, Instagram, Telegram and WhatsApp rather than in apps. I could not get Google Trends volumes for specific queries, so search behaviour is shown indirectly, through the content genres publishers produce every exam cycle.

### Cited Findings
**Phones are the study device, but social use dominates**
- ASER 2023 "Beyond Basics" (rural youth aged 14-18; 34,745 respondents in 28 districts across 26 states):
  - About 89% have a smartphone at home and about 92% can use one.
  - About two-thirds use the phone for study: watching study videos, solving doubts or exchanging notes.
  - Only about half of social-media users knew the online safety settings, and males were more likely to know them.
  - Sources: [ASER 2023 main findings (PDF)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2023-Main-Findings_Jan-16-2024-1.pdf); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/girls-in-rural-india-less-likely-to-know-how-to-use-smartphones-computers-survey-93960); [Rau's IAS summary](https://compass.rauias.com/current-affairs/beyond-basics-aser-2023/). Figures come from secondary coverage; the PDF host was blocked.
- ASER 2024 (digital module, 1,14,553 rural youth aged 14-16):
  - About 90% have a smartphone at home and about 82% know how to use one.
  - 57% used it for educational activities, against 76% for social media.
  - Social-media use was 78.8% among boys and 73.4% among girls.
  - Sources: [Vision IAS summary](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-01-29/society/annual-status-of-education-report-aser-2024-released-by-ngo-pratham-foundation); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/news/smartphone-access-education-recovery-in-aser-2024-1857277); [Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/human-development/youth-s-digital-readiness-in-rural-india.html).
- An opinion piece on ASER 2024 notes no gender gap in education-related digital activities. It also says digital-safety knowledge is weak, and girls are more vulnerable online — [Deccan Chronicle opinion](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/opinion/columnists/deepak-kumar-smartphone-skills-growing-as-digital-divide-gets-bridged-across-rural-india-1857274).

**The marks, percentile and rank obsession**
- Every JEE cycle, coaching and portal sites publish "marks vs percentile vs rank" tables. The conversions are estimates and differ between sites and years — [PW](https://www.pw.live/iit-jee/exams/jee-main-marks-vs-percentile); [Aakash](https://www.aakash.ac.in/jee/jee-main-marks-vs-percentile); [eSaral](https://www.esaral.com/jee/jee-main-marks-vs-rank); [Unacademy](https://unacademy.com/content/jee-main-marks-vs-percentile/).
- After JEE Main 2026, the National Testing Agency (NTA) itself published an explainer on percentile vs marks. Its data showed a 31-mark gap across shifts, so the same raw score maps to different percentiles depending on shift difficulty (normalisation) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/nta-explains-percentile-vs-marks-after-jee-main-2026-data-shows-31-mark-gap-across-shifts-highlights-how-normalisation-balances-tough-and-easy-papers); [PW explainer](https://www.pw.live/news/jee-main-result-2026-how-nta-converts-marks-into-percentile-and-rank).

**Topper culture persists even where boards discourage it**
- CBSE does not publish a merit or toppers list, to reduce unhealthy competition. Instead it gives merit certificates to the top 0.1% in each subject through DigiLocker — [PW news](https://www.pw.live/news/cbse-class-10-12-result-2026-no-topper-list-policy-explained); [Punekar News](https://www.punekarnews.in/cbse-class-10-results-likely-by-may-20-confirms-no-toppers-list/).
- Media and coaching sites still publish unofficial toppers lists. After the first CBSE Class 10 exam of 2026, 55,368 students scored 95% or more — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/class-10-cbse-results-2026-toppers-55368-score-95-or-above-no-merit-list-these-students-have-excelled-kvs-jnvs-region-wise-list/amp). Coaching blogs also run "who got 500 out of 500" posts — [PW Vidyapeeth blog](https://www.pw.live/vp-blogs/exams/who-got-500-out-of-500-in-10th-cbse-result-2026).
- CBSE publishes toppers' answer sheets ("model answers"). Students download them to learn how to structure answers to the marking scheme and word limits, and portals re-host them every year — [Careers360 (Class 12 topper answer sheets)](https://school.careers360.com/articles/cbse-class-12-topper-answer-sheet); [Careers360 (model answers news, 2018, older)](https://news.careers360.com/cbse-10th-12th-model-answers-2018-released-know-what-expected-you).

**Study-with-me, studygram and studytube**
- ThePrint (2022) reported that Indians "go live" while they study. Its examples:
  - NEET aspirant Varsha Rana started YouTube study-with-me sessions in Dec 2020 for company and consistency.
  - Viewers include NEET, IIT-JEE, UPSC, GATE, commerce and humanities students.
  - #studygram had 16.4M posts by Sept 2022. That is a global hashtag count, not India-only.
  - Source: [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/pen-paper-phone-indians-are-now-going-live-when-they-study-its-called-studygram/1130627).
- Critics say the aesthetic turns study into a performance, or "toxic productivity" — [Youth Time](https://youthtimemag.com/studytube-studytok-studygram-toxic-productivity-the-youth); [Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/hans/young-hans/the-rise-of-studytube-and-studygram-where-focus-meets-aesthetic-983053).

**Telegram and WhatsApp as the study back-channel**
- Telegram became a go-to test-prep platform with thousands of community groups for exam notifications, handwritten notes and books. It also has a piracy problem (older report, pre-2021) — [FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/telegram-turns-go-to-platform-for-test-prep-in-india-but-has-a-piracy-problem/).
- Before every major exam, Telegram groups carry messages claiming to have the leaked paper — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-ugc-net-2024-scam-paper-leak-telegram-link-app-nlu-odisha-nsliu-bangalore-cyber-law-india-office-experts).
- A lockdown-era Vidyasaarathi survey of 10,000+ students found 79% used smartphones to study online. 59% preferred WhatsApp and Zoom calls for online classes (older, 2020) — [Digital Terminal](https://digitalterminal.in/news/20221.html).

**Aspirational stories that get shared**
- Ritika Pal's family sold her mother's jewellery. She bought biology books, relied on YouTube and cleared NEET 2021 without coaching (SC-category rank 3,032) — [DNA](https://www.dnaindia.com/education/report-meet-woman-ritika-pal-whose-mother-sold-jewellery-to-support-her-education-later-cracked-neet-without-coaching-with-air-3032-3120477); [The Logical Indian](https://thelogicalindian.com/trending/neet-aspirant-youtube-coaching-ritika-31889).
- Kashmiri daily-wage labourer Umer Ahmad Ganie cracked NEET without coaching — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/325808/kashmir-daily-wager-umer-ahmad-ganie-cracked-neet-exam-without-coaching-break-poverty).
- *12th Fail* (2023) is a biopic of a UPSC aspirant, shot in Delhi's Mukherjee Nagar with real aspirants as background extras.
  - Aspirants told ThePrint they cried watching it; the teaser was screened for UPSC students.
  - It won Best Film at the 69th Filmfare Awards.
  - Sources: [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/beyond-the-reel/with-12th-fail-bollywood-discovers-mukherjee-nagar-stories-classes-clamour-commerce/1829075); [Filmibeat](https://filmibeat.com/bollywood/news/2023/vidhu-vinod-chopras-12th-fail-teaser-earns-praise-from-upsc-students-for-compelling-storyline-360739.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Fail).

**What Google says India searched (2025)**
- In India's Year in Search 2025, IPL was the #1 trending search and Google Gemini was #2. "Education Portal 3.0" appeared among news searches. No exam category surfaced in the coverage — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/explore-communicate/indias-year-in-search-2025/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/googles-2025-search-trends-operation-sindoor-saiyaara-dharmendras-death-heres-what-captured-indias-attention-this-year-505034-2025-12-04).

### Inferences
- The most curious moments are translation moments ("what do my marks mean as percentile, rank, seat?") and "what did the topper do" moments. Both are comparative by nature. An app can answer the curiosity privately, in ranges and focused on process, without the public comparison.
- Topper answer sheets are official, legitimate and under-used. Students already hunt for them, and they teach format rather than rank.
- Study-with-me formats meet a need for company and consistency (body-doubling), not a need for content.
- Rumours and pirated material flow through Telegram and WhatsApp, which leaves room for a trusted "official source" layer.
- AI is now mainstream (Gemini was India's #2 trending search), so "AI" alone is not a hook. Exam-system knowledge specific to India is.

### Gaps
- Could not query Google Trends. I found no published India-level search volumes for "rank predictor", "college predictor", "previous year questions", "marks vs rank", "topper notes", "how to study" or "board exam tips". The evidence here is indirect: how much content of each type gets published, and how often result-day sites crash.
- No data found on exam-meme pages, view counts for topper-interview videos, or the Instagram reach of Indian study creators.
- ASER figures came through secondary sources because asercentre.org was blocked.

## 2. Tools that went viral among Indian students, and what drives traffic spikes

### Takeaway
The tools that spread are free, mass-market and tied to exam moments:
- YouTube-first teachers, led by PhysicsWallah's network of about 99M subscribers.
- Photo-based doubt solvers such as Doubtnut and Gauth.
- Govt-exam mock-test platforms such as Adda247 and Testbook.
- Predictor, answer-key and result pages, which spike on answer-key and result days, often while official servers crash.

Free general AI (ChatGPT, Gemini) is now a default study tool for many students.

  - Sources: [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/09/edtech-physicswallah-hybrid-model-fuels-3000-crore-growth-ipo); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/ipo/physics-wallah-ipo-know-about-the-firm-s-business-model-strengths-and-risks-ahead-of-public-issue/article-184307).

### Inferences
- Three things drove these tools' spread:
  - free content in Hindi or Hinglish from a charismatic teacher (PW);
  - instant answers to a photo (Doubtnut, Gauth);
  - "what does my number mean" tools on high-anxiety days.
- Kelvra already covers instant answers. The "what does my number mean" moment is the largest unused traffic spike, and it needs a responsible design.
- When official sites fail on result or answer-key day, students get hours of panicked searching. A calm, accurate companion is valuable at exactly that moment.
- Testbook's one-time-user pattern shows that exam-only tools churn after the exam. Features tied to what comes after (counselling, Plan B, the next exam) could extend retention.
- Gemini and ChatGPT are free, so Kelvra's edge has to be specificity: Indian board and NTA formats, official artefacts, counselling rules, language support, low data use and safety.

### Gaps
- No public traffic numbers for Careers360, Shiksha or Collegedunia predictors, or for mock-test leaderboards on Testbook, Embibe or PW.
- Unacademy live-class trends were not researched.
- No India-specific Gauth data; Doubtnut's figures are dated.

## 3. Language: Hindi, regional languages and Hinglish

### Takeaway
Almost all Indian internet users (98%) consume content in Indic languages, but high-stakes exam answering still leans English. About 79% of NEET 2026 candidates chose English and about 15% Hindi; regional languages are small, and some are shrinking. The useful middle ground for learning is likely bilingual or Hinglish explanation that keeps English exam terms. Most evidence for Hinglish preference comes from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- IAMAI-Kantar "Internet in India 2024":
  - 886M active internet users, of whom 488M (55%) are rural.
  - 98% (about 870M) access content in Indic languages; Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam are the most popular.
  - 57% of urban users prefer regional-language content.
  - 41% of the population is still offline. Reasons given: awareness 25%, affordability 16%, lack of local-language content 13%.
  - Sources: [IAMAI-Kantar report (PDF)](https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar_%20IAMAI%20report_2024_.pdf); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/indias-internet-revolution-key-insights-from-kantar-and-iamai-report-461043-2025-01-16); [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/india-news/india-s-internet-users-to-exceed-900-mn-in-2025-driven-by-indic-languages-125011600835_1.html).
- NEET-UG is offered in 13 languages — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/education/medical-admissions/neet-2025-in-13-languages-regional-preferences-surge-as-english-sees-decline-144922).
- NEET-UG 2026 language choices, from NTA data reported by FPJ:
  - English 18,08,535 and Hindi 3,45,247.
  - Marathi only 1,144 (927 in 2025; 1,759 in 2024), even though Maharashtra has one of the largest applicant pools.
  - Source: [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/neet-ug-2026-just-1144-candidates-chose-marathi-medium-despite-maharashtra-having-one-of-indias-largest-neet-applicant-pools).
  - My calc, against roughly 22.8 lakh candidates: English about 79%, Hindi about 15%, and the other 11 languages together about 5-6%. The denominator is approximate.
- Hindi-medium NEET registrations rose from 1,79,857 (2019) to about 3,57,908 (2024), according to Medical Dialogues. DT Next places the 3.45 lakh figure differently, so treat year-by-year Hindi counts as unconfirmed — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/education/medical-admissions/neet-2025-in-13-languages-regional-preferences-surge-as-english-sees-decline-144922); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/neet-in-tamil-declines-as-mother-tongue-loses-ground-nationwide).
- Tamil-medium NEET candidates went from 36,335 (2024) to 26,580 (2025), then 29,845 (2026) — [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/neet-in-tamil-declines-as-mother-tongue-loses-ground-nationwide). FPJ's headline: "Despite NEP, English still the most preferred language in competitive exams" — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/despite-nep-english-still-the-most-preferred-language-in-competitive-exams).
- Hinglish and vernacular evidence (all vendor sources):
  - Lido Learning launched Hinglish live tutorials. It said students in both metros and smaller cities are more comfortable in Hinglish and struggle with complex concepts taught in English (c. 2020) — [India Education Diary](https://indiaeducationdiary.in/?p=37972).
  - Vidyakul teaches state-board students in Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi and Hinglish — [Vidyakul](https://vidyakul.com/aboutus).
  - EduGorilla says over 70% of its students are rural and planned to expand into nine vernacular languages — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/edugorilla-raises-inr-165-crore-funding-led-by-sucseed/429013).
- Doubtnut's user base was over 85% outside the top-10 cities (older) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1940693).

### Inferences
- "Learn in my language, answer in the exam's language" is likely the right model. That means bilingual explanations that keep English technical terms, plus a Hindi-English term glossary, rather than full translation.
- Hindi is clearly the second language of competitive exams. Kelvra's Hindi family updates already fit this; the AI tutor and voice doubts should also handle Hindi-English code-mixing.
- Regional-language exam-takers are a small share, and some languages are shrinking. For regional languages, parent messages and plain explanations matter more than exam-medium content.

### Gaps
- No recent independent survey on Hinglish preference among students.
- The KPMG-Google "Indian Languages" report (2017) was not retrieved, and it would be dated anyway.
- I could not verify the share of students in English-medium schools; sources conflict (about 17% vs 26%).
- No split of PW's audience by Hindi vs English found. JEE Main language-wise data not found.

## 4. Device and data realities

### Takeaway
Most students have a phone at home, but many do not own one, especially girls, and family phones are shared. Mobile data has become more expensive after the 2024-25 tariff increases, and phone prices are rising because of a global memory shortage. Designing for low data, shared phones and offline use is a real differentiator.

### Cited Findings
- ASER 2023: among youth who can use a smartphone, 43.7% of males own one, against 19.8% of females. Only about 9% of households have a computer or laptop — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/girls-in-rural-india-less-likely-to-know-how-to-use-smartphones-computers-survey-93960).
- ASER 2024 (ages 14-16): 36.2% of boys and 26.9% of girls own a smartphone. Bihar, Jharkhand and MP lag on both ownership and skills — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2025-01-29/society/annual-status-of-education-report-aser-2024-released-by-ngo-pratham-foundation); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/news/smartphone-access-education-recovery-in-aser-2024-1857277).
- IAMAI-Kantar 2024: women are 58% of shared-device internet users in rural India — [IAMAI-Kantar PDF](https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar_%20IAMAI%20report_2024_.pdf); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/surge-in-indic-language-adoption-leads-to-55-surge-in-rural-internet-users-53632.htm).
- Data prices:

- Roughly 78% of new phones still cost under ₹30,000 (the inverse of the 22% premium share), and entry-tier phones are being squeezed. Many students will keep older, low-RAM phones for longer, so app size, offline packs and battery use matter.
- Shared phones, especially girls', call for a PIN-locked student profile, discreet notifications, no lock-screen previews of study or stress content, and a low-storage mode.
- Rising data costs favour downloadable study kits, text-first answers and light WhatsApp interactions over video.

## 6. What parents are curious about

### Takeaway
Parents' worries cluster around four things:
- whether their child is actually studying or is addicted to the phone;
- whether paid coaching or edtech is worth the money and can be trusted;
- how the exam system and results work;
- how their child compares with peers.

The state tries to channel these worries through Pariksha Pe Charcha and CBSE counselling for parents. Kota officials repeatedly cite parental pressure as a factor in student suicides.

### Cited Findings
- LocalCircles, Nov-Dec 2025 (urban parents of 9-17-year-olds, large online sample):
  - 2 in 3 say their children are addicted to social media, videos or online gaming.
  - About 61% report impatience, 58% aggression, 50% hyperactivity and 47% lethargy.

These are self-selected online panels, not nationally representative.
- Trust in coaching and edtech:

- "Is my child studying?" is driven by fear of phone addiction as much as by scores. A student-controlled signal of effort and consistency, not rank, could answer it while reducing surveillance pressure.

### Gaps

## 7. Calendar and cultural moments

### Takeaway

- Pariksha Pe Charcha registration (Dec-Jan);

## 8. Risks and how a responsible app should handle them

### Takeaway
The risk environment hardened between 2024 and 2026:
- record student suicides (13,892 in 2023);
- binding Supreme Court guidelines against sorting students into batches by performance and against public shaming (July 2025);
- consumer-regulator (CCPA) fines for misleading topper claims;
- two NEET paper-leak crises, including the 2026 cancellation that affected about 22.79 lakh candidates;
- a steady flow of fake-leak rumours.

An app that ranks students publicly, shames them or amplifies rumours is now ethically exposed, and possibly legally exposed too.

### Cited Findings
- NCRB's ADSI 2023 report (published 29 Sep 2025) recorded 13,892 student suicides:
  - the highest in a decade, and 8.1% of all suicides (13,044 in 2022);
  - Maharashtra 14.7%, MP 10.5%, UP 9.9%, TN 9.6% of the total;
  - Source: [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/ncrb-report-2023-over-13800-student-suicide-deaths-highest-in-10-years-national-crime-records-bureau-maharashtra-up-tn/amp).
  - About a 65% rise over 10 years — [Careers360 Hindi](https://hindinews.careers360.com/student-suicide-cases-in-india-increased-by-65-percent-in-last-10-years-claims-ncrb-report/amp).
- Kota 2023:
  - Record student suicides: 26 in year-end reports, though counts range from 23 to 29.
  - The district stopped coaching tests for two months and required anti-hanging devices on hostel fans.
  - There is a shortage of psychologists for about 2.5 lakh students.
  - Sources: [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/kota-factory-in-2023-year-of-highest-student-suicides-desperate-preventive-measures/1906181); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1055031/scroll_in); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/kotas-student-mental-health-crisis-unmet-shortage-of-psychologists-despite-rising-suicides).
- Supreme Court, *Sukdeb Saha v. State of AP* (25 July 2025):
  - 15 binding guidelines for all educational institutions, including coaching centres.
  - Institutions shall "refrain from engaging in batch segregation based on academic performance, public shaming, or assignment of academic targets disproportionate" to a student's capacity.
  - States were told to notify coaching regulations within two months.
  - Sources: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/supreme-court/in-neet-aspirants-death-case-supreme-court-issues-guidelines-to-protect-mental-health-of-students-in-colleges-coaching-centres-298795); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/sukdeb-saha-v-the-state-of-andhra-pradesh-2025-insc-893-mental-health-policy-educational-institutions-1586163); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/sc-issues-pan-india-guidelines-to-deal-with-suicides-in-educational-institutions).
- CBSE's no-merit-list policy exists to reduce unhealthy competition — [PW](https://www.pw.live/news/cbse-class-10-12-result-2026-no-topper-list-policy-explained).
- The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) is acting on misleading topper and result claims:
  - It has issued 60+ notices — [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2086948).
  - Vision IAS was fined ₹11 lakh, the first penalty for a repeat offence — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/business/ccpa-fines-vision-ias-11-lakh-for-repeat-misleading-upsc-ads-first-penalty-for-second-offence).

### Inferences
Guardrails Kelvra should adopt:
- No public leaderboards and nothing that works like batch segregation for minors.
- Any rank or percentile output stays private, is shown as a range with its uncertainty, and is never shared with parents automatically. This follows the spirit of the SC guidelines and CBSE's no-merit-list policy.
- No topper names or photos in marketing, and no outcome claims, to stay clear of CCPA action.
- Rumour handling links only to official notices. Kelvra never hosts or forwards "leaked" or "guess" papers, and labels unverified claims clearly.
- Result-day crisis protocol: safe-messaging language, visible helplines (for example CBSE's 1800-11-8004) and escalation to a human.
- Privacy on shared phones, and no direct messages from strangers to minors.

**H1. Rank Reality Check (answer-key day: private score, then a percentile and rank *range*)**
- *Idea:* On answer-key day the student enters their responses. Kelvra computes the score, explains normalisation for their shift, and shows a percentile and rank band with its uncertainty. It then maps that band to realistic options, including a Plan B.
- *Why curious:* This is the most-asked question of every cycle, the tables online disagree, and official sites crash.
- *Evidence:*
  - The 31-mark shift gap — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/nta-explains-percentile-vs-marks-after-jee-main-2026-data-shows-31-mark-gap-across-shifts-highlights-how-normalisation-balances-tough-and-easy-papers).
  - Every major coaching site publishes its own conversion table — [PW](https://www.pw.live/iit-jee/exams/jee-main-marks-vs-percentile).
  - Existing predictors rely on previous years' data — [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/question-how-accurate-is-the-college-or-rank-predictor-is-of-career-360).
  - Result-day crashes — [Aakash](https://www.aakash.ac.in/blog/jee-main-result-2026-link-not-working-scorecard-not-opening/).
- *Risk:* rank obsession, false precision, and parents seeing a number.
- *Guardrails:*
  - Show ranges only, plus last year's error band.
  - Label it "not official".
  - Keep it private by default and never send it in the WhatsApp family update without the student choosing to.
  - Every band comes with "options exist" next steps.

**H2. Counselling Copilot (JoSAA, MCC, CUET and state rounds)**
- *Idea:* A choice-filling simulator with round-by-round explanations (float, slide, freeze), deadline reminders, a document checklist, and "what happens if I reject this seat".
- *Why curious:* Rounds run for weeks and confuse families; portals run live blogs at every round; exam-only apps lose their users after the exam.
- *Evidence:*

- *Risk:* wrong advice can cost a seat; "guaranteed seat" claims would invite CCPA action.

- *Idea:* The student photographs their answer and Kelvra compares its structure (intro, keywords, diagrams, steps, word limit) with official topper answer sheets for the same question type. It shows what examiners reward, not "you vs topper".

**H14. Result-Day Companion**
- *Idea:* A countdown plan covering:
  - where and when to check the result (the official site, DigiLocker or UMANG) and what to do if the site crashes;
  - pre-written messages to family;
  - "every score has a next step" option maps;
  - one-tap access to helplines (for example CBSE's 1800-11-8004) and a trusted adult.
- *Why curious:* This is the most emotional day of the year, and the official sites often fail on it.
- *Evidence:*
  - Result-day crashes — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/re-neet-2026-result-server-issue).

- *Risk:* the highest of any hook.
- *Guardrails:*
  - Clinical review of the copy and safe-messaging rules.

**H15. IPL- and Festival-Aware Planner**

- *Why curious:* It speaks in India's cultural rhythm, and a "study through the IPL" challenge is natural content to share.
- *Evidence:*
  - IPL was India's #1 trending search in 2025 — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/explore-communicate/indias-year-in-search-2025/).

**Prioritisation (my judgement)**
1. H4 (Rumour Shield) and H8 (Shared-Phone and Low-Data Mode): cheapest to build, easy to share over WhatsApp, and they build trust with parents.
2. H1 and H2 (Rank Reality Check and Counselling Copilot): catch the biggest predictable traffic spikes (April-July) and fight churn after the exam.
3. H3 (Topper Copy X-Ray): a board-season (Feb-Mar) hook that uses official artefacts and strengthens the existing answer-writing coach.
4. H14 (Result-Day Companion): sets the safety standard and earns press goodwill. Build it with clinical review before launch.

**Design rules across all hooks**
- Private by default; show ranges, not single points.
- Measure effort, not rank; no public leaderboards for minors.
- Use official sources only and never touch "leaked" material.
- Bilingual by default.
- Low data use and safe on shared phones.
- No outcome claims and no topper images in marketing (CCPA).
- Crisis routing on every high-stakes screen.

### Gaps
