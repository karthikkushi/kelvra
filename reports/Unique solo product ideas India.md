# Sell to tutors, not to students

The best idea in the research is not a new student app. It is a tool for small tuition teachers: turn the teacher's own paper into a phone quiz, check handwritten test sheets from photos, and send each parent a clear report. Tutors already pay Rs 500 to Rs 32,000 a year for software, and the two big funded companies have left the small tutor behind. Students in India pay almost nothing, and ChatGPT Go and Gemini Pro were given away free in India for 12 to 18 months ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/apps/chatgpt-gemini-and-perplexity-pro-paid-subscriptions-are-now-free-in-india-heres-how-to-get-them.html)). Your current app sits in the exact group that free AI now covers: flashcards, quizzes and study guides ([Edtech Insiders](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/study-modes-the-new-ai-battleground)). Most of your code can be reused for the tutor tool, so this is a change of customer, not a restart. The second-best idea is a new product: a trusted rules-and-deadlines guide for college counselling, where families pay Rs 2,000 to Rs 50,000 today. Please read this with care: the researchers could mostly read only search-result summaries, not full pages. One notes file (student_parent_unmet_pains.md) may be incomplete because its researcher was cut off. No idea below has proof that customers will pay you. Each one needs the cheap test described before you build.

## Free AI has taken the student, but not the tutor

The evidence says three things. Together they point away from another student app.

First, the labs now give away your core features. ChatGPT Study Mode launched in July 2025 and Gemini Guided Learning in August 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)). Chegg lost 30% of its revenue in one year and cut staff twice ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/chegg-q1-2025-revenue-down-30-as-company-restructures-and-explores-strategic-alternatives)). Products that take an input and give back a generated answer, with nothing else, are the ones that died. Products that survived own something else: a daily habit, a group, a channel like WhatsApp, or data the chatbot does not have. One Indian example, SkillBytes, says it has 800,000 students on WhatsApp, but this number is self-reported ([CMOtech](https://cmotech.news/story/skillbytes-hits-800-000-students-on-whatsapp-in-india)).

Second, Indian consumers rarely pay for apps. **Only about 1.4% of app installs in India and Southeast Asia become paying users, and a paying user is worth about USD 14 in the first year** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). By the researchers' arithmetic, Rs 1 lakh a month needs about 70,000 installs a year. The Indian consumer apps that do earn (astrology, audio stories, short video) spend almost as much on ads as they earn. Seekho spent Rs 134 crore on ads to earn Rs 142 crore ([Medial/Entrackr](https://medial.app/news/seekho-spends-rs-134-cr-on-advertising-for-rs-142-cr-revenue-in-fy25-4706ea50f7972)). UPI AutoPay also fails often: 55% to 90% of debits failed in August 2025 ([Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/Mint-New-Delhi/UPI-AUTOPAYS-ENDLESS-WOES-FORCING-AN-INDUSTRY-RETHINK)). The researchers found no public case of a solo Indian developer earning good recurring money from Indian consumers. That absence is a signal, though Indian founders also publish less.

Third, people who earn from students do pay. A government survey found that **27% of Indian school students take private coaching** ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/27-of-students-rely-on-private-coaching-urban-participation-higher-cms-education-survey-2025)). Tutors buy test-paper packs from Examin8 for Rs 500 to Rs 10,000 a year ([Examin8](https://examin8.com/)). Classplus sells a branded app for about Rs 20,000 to Rs 32,000 a year, which reviewers call too costly for small tutors ([Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/208852/class-plus); [GeekChamp](https://geekchamp.com/classplus-pricing-reviews-2026/)). Classplus now leans on its test-prep business, and Teachmint earns from classroom hardware ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/from-enabler-to-aggregator-classplus-flips-its-edtech-playbook/); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)). There is a price gap between Rs 3,000 and Rs 10,000 a year that nobody serious fills. A solo developer with near-zero costs can live on a segment that is too small for a funded company. For scale, 300 tutors paying Rs 6,000 a year is Rs 18 lakh a year.

## Seven ideas, ranked by evidence and effort

The ranking weighs four things: proof that someone pays, how much of your code is reused, how you reach users with no budget, and legal risk. "Evidence" here means evidence of demand. It does not mean proof that your product will sell.

| Rank | Idea | Reuses your app or new? | Who pays | Demand evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tutor test loop: paper to phone quiz, photo checking, parent report | Reuses most of it | Tutor, yearly | Medium to strong |
| 2 | Counselling rules-and-deadlines guide, with a "is this notice real?" checker as the free door | New product; reuses parent explainers | Parent, once per season; or counsellors | Strong for demand, weak for your chance to win |
| 3 | Printable mistake sheets and parent photo-check of worksheets | Reuses mistake notebook | Tutor (bundled) or parent | Medium abroad, none in India |
| 4 | CBSE answer-book re-evaluation helper | Reuses answer-writing coach | Student, once | Weak, one year of data |
| 5 | Small-group study timer with exam recap | Reuses focus tools and puzzle | Nobody at first; growth feature | Medium for use, weak for money |
| 6 | Family referee pledge (no money) | Reuses WhatsApp family update | Nobody; retention feature | Weak |
| 7 | Indian exam data inside ChatGPT and Claude | New; small | Nobody; distribution bet | Weak |

### 1. Tutor test loop (top recommendation)

The product does three jobs for a tutor with 30 to 200 students. The tutor photographs a handwritten question paper, and the app turns it into a quiz. Students open a link from the tutor's WhatsApp group and play on their own phone that night. The next day the tutor posts the results. For paper tests, the tutor photographs the answer sheets, the AI drafts marks and feedback, and the tutor approves or changes them. Each parent then gets a short report with the tutor's name on it. Quizizz and Kahoot assume a classroom with a projector. The researchers found no tool built for the tutor with only a WhatsApp group. Photo checking exists (ClassPulse, Eklavvya) but every vendor hides its price and sells by sales call ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/classpulse); [Eklavvya](https://www.eklavvya.com/ai-grading-coaching-institute/)). Free ChatGPT can write questions, but it does not run a class, keep each student's history, or send a parent report. That packaged loop is the product, not the question writing.

**Distribution:** the tutor is your channel. Each tutor brings 30 to 200 students and their parents. Start with tutors in your own city, tutor groups on WhatsApp, Telegram and Facebook, and tutors listed on UrbanPro. Tutors already pay UrbanPro for leads and complain about lead quality ([FindMyGuru](https://www.findmyguru.com/compare/findmyguru-vs-urbanpro)). A branded parent report is also the tutor's own advertisement, which helps you sell it. **Money:** a yearly prepaid plan of about Rs 5,000 to Rs 12,000, plus a small per-page fee for checking. These prices are the researchers' estimate, not tested. Annual prepay is normal in India and avoids AutoPay failures. **First version (2 to 4 weeks):** only Class 9 and 10, one board, science, social science and English. Build photo-to-quiz, the share link, the results board and the parent report. Your quiz engine, answer-writing coach, parent explainers and WhatsApp update already do most of this. Leave maths and photo checking of long answers for later. **Cheap test first:** talk to 15 to 20 tutors. Then check one real test for free by hand, using the AI yourself behind the scenes, and send the reports. Ask for payment for the next test. If fewer than 3 to 5 tutors pay within two or three weeks, stop.

**Risks, stated plainly.** No source proves that small centres will pay for checking. Handwriting accuracy is the main technical risk. Independent tests show AI still reads Devanagari handwriting badly ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29213)), so start with English-medium answers and always keep the teacher's approval step. Groq appears to have retired its vision model, so plan image reading on Gemini Flash-Lite or Sarvam Vision at about Rs 0.50 a page ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/sarvam-ai-slashes-vision-api-pricing-by-67-bringing-document-digitisation-within-reach-for-indian-enterprises)). Many solo developers are building tutor tools in 2026, so the scarce thing is access to tutors, not code. Children's data law applies: see the legal section below.

### 2. Counselling guide with a real-or-fake notice checker

This is the strongest demand in all the notes. After JEE and NEET, families face one-time choices that cannot be undone. NEET has a central process plus separate state processes, each with its own rules, bonds and penalties ([Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/neet-state-counselling-2026)). Consultants charge Rs 25,000 to Rs 50,000, and one sells an alerts-only pack for Rs 2,000 ([neetugguidance.in](https://www.neetugguidance.in/online-booking.php)). Fraud is common: three Mumbai students lost Rs 18.58 lakh to fake counsellors found on Instagram ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-crime-3-students-duped-of-1858-lakh-in-fake-mbbs-bams-admission-scam-powai-police-register-case)). CBSE had to deny at least four fake circulars in 2026 ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/fact-check-has-cbse-issued-re-evaluation-cancellation-notice-heres-the-truth-behind-viral-social-media-claims)). A general chatbot fails here because the rules change every year and even specialist sites disagree with each other.

The product is a dated, source-linked guide for one narrow case, for example NEET counselling in one or two states. It shows a bond and penalty table, a deadline tracker, and plain Hindi and English explanations with the official link beside every answer. The free door is a checker where a parent forwards a notice and learns if it is real. **Distribution:** copy Delta Education. Two students ran a free Telegram group answering admission questions and reached Rs 10 lakh a year ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/from-telegram-group-to-a-profitable-startup-student-turns-admission-queries-into-rs10-lakh-business-556058-2026-09-20)). The checker also spreads on WhatsApp, the same place rumours spread. **Money:** a one-time Rs 199 to Rs 999 season pass paid by plain UPI. A second option is a workbench for independent counsellors at perhaps Rs 5,000 to Rs 20,000 a season; no such tool was found, but that is weak evidence. **First version:** the notice checker and one state's rules table, run partly by hand. **Cheap test:** start the free Telegram or WhatsApp group now, in November, before the January to June 2027 exam season. Sell a Rs 199 pre-order for the alerts.

**Risks.** The work is seasonal, mostly May to October. A wrong date or rule can cost a family lakhs, so you carry real responsibility. Never promise a seat. PW, Allen and Careers360 already sell predictors, so do not build a rank predictor. The category has a scam reputation that you will share. This is a new product. Only your Hindi and English parent explainers carry over.

### 3. Printable mistake sheets and parent photo-check

In China, one app let a parent check a child's whole arithmetic worksheet with one photo. It claimed 70 million sums checked a day, but that is a company claim from 2019 ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/start-ups/article/2185452/tencent-backed-ai-firm-aims-free-parents-and-teachers-checking)). Chinese learning tablets also print practice sheets made from the child's own mistakes. The researchers found no Indian product for either, which means "none appeared in search", not "none exists". The print sheet is a few days of work for you: a one-page PDF of ten questions from the mistake notebook, with a QR code for answers. Indian parents distrust screen time and print shops are everywhere. **Distribution and money:** on its own, this depends on parents paying, which is the weak point in India. Bundle it into idea 1, so the tutor hands each student a personal sheet. **Cheap test:** make sheets by hand for ten students of one tutor and see if they come back completed. **Risks:** handwriting accuracy for the photo-check, so let the app compute right and wrong for sums and use the AI only to read digits.

### 4. CBSE re-evaluation helper

In 2026 CBSE marked Class 12 papers on screen for the first time, and many students reported low marks ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/05/30/cbse-class-12-osm-controversy.amp.html)). Re-evaluation costs Rs 100 per question and about 40,000 students used the process ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/cbse-says-40000-students-complete-re-evaluation-process-without-issues-4025701)). The idea: the student uploads the scanned answer book, and the tool compares it with the official marking scheme and suggests which questions are worth a challenge. This reuses your answer-writing coach. It is unusual, and no paid service was found. But the evidence is weak. It rests on one year, the window is a few weeks, and 40,000 is a small market. Build it only as a seasonal feature in May 2027, after a free test on a few real answer books. Present every result as an opinion, never as a promise of more marks.

### 5 to 7. Useful features, but they will not earn money

**Small-group study timer with an exam recap (reuses focus tools).** A Korean app, YPT, claims 5 million users for a study timer that shows which friends are studying now. Indian JEE and NEET students already use it ([App Store India](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/ypt-yeolpumta/id1441909643)). Indian copies exist but look small. A shareable "your exam prep, wrapped" card copies Spotify Wrapped. This is a retention and sharing feature for your current app. No source shows students paying for it. Test it with one WhatsApp study group before building.

**Family referee pledge (reuses the WhatsApp family update).** Research on 75,828 goals at stickK shows that a human referee raises success ([KAIST paper](https://ic.kaist.ac.kr/files/papers/lee2019commitment.pdf)). A student sets a weekly goal and a parent or elder sibling confirms it. Do not add a cash deposit. That version sits close to the gaming law.

**Indian exam data inside ChatGPT and Claude (new, small).** Solo developers can list apps in ChatGPT's directory, but there is no revenue share ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-now-accepting-chatgpt-app-submissions-from-third-party-devs-launches); [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/chatgpt-app-monetization-apps-sdk/1372343.md)). Treat it only as free distribution for idea 2. Nobody was found earning from it.

## Popular ideas the evidence says to avoid

**Another AI tutor, doubt solver or flashcard maker.** Free lab tools cover it, and paid AI tiers were free in India. **Rank predictors.** Big players give them away, and they need crowd data you do not have. **Cash-prize quizzes, fantasy formats or money stakes.** India's 2025 online gaming law bans any game where money is deposited in hope of a money return, whatever the skill involved ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/indias-online-gaming-reset-decoding-proga-and-the-2026-rules/)). Dream11 said it lost about 95% of revenue ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/08/dream11-loses-95-revenue-gaming-ban-wont-cut-jobs-winzo)). Cash trivia apps also faded on their own before the law. **Anonymous polls among classmates.** Gas was shut down, and the US regulator banned NGL from serving minors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/ftc-bans-ngl-from-offering-its-anonymous-social-app-to-minors)). **Fee and attendance trackers for tutors.** There are many free hobby clones. Do not lead with this. **School ERPs, UPSC answer evaluation, study-abroad tools, and apps for gyms, salons or clinics.** All are crowded, and UPSC AI evaluation is racing to free. **JEE and NEET senior mentoring.** PhysicsWallah and others already do it. **A Rs 1 trial that turns into auto-debit.** It has drawn a complaint to the RBI and is risky for an unknown brand ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-auto-debit-scam-kuku-fm-stage-paytm-google-pay-phonepe-how-cancel-auto-debit)). **A large WhatsApp chatbot.** Several reports say Meta started charging for more message types from 1 October 2026 ([AiSensy](https://m.aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-pricing-update-october-2026/)). No official Meta page was read, so check this. The safer design is that the tutor or student shares links and images in their own WhatsApp, which costs nothing. **AI running on cheap Android phones.** Chrome's built-in AI does not support Android ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api)).

## The law and your stack both need attention now

**Children's data.** India's data protection rules were notified in November 2025. They require verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18, and a checkbox is not enough ([Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-design-a-verifiable-parental-consent-process-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025)). Tracking, behaviour monitoring and targeted ads aimed at children are barred, and parental consent does not remove this bar ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-children-s-data-are-you-compliant)). Schools have a narrow exemption. It is unknown if a private app or a tutor can use it, so assume not. This matters for your current app too. Focus and habit tracking for under-18 users looks like behaviour monitoring. That is this report's reading, not a legal ruling. One source says the child rules take effect after an 18-month phase-in, which needs checking. The safest design makes the parent or tutor the account holder, keeps groups private, shows no ads, uses nicknames on any leaderboard, and deletes photos after processing. The researchers did not read the law itself, only law-firm summaries. Ask a lawyer before you hold data on many children.

**Online gaming.** Your daily puzzle is safe while it has no money in and no money out. Points must never convert to cash, vouchers or anything tradable, because redeemable rewards are one thing the regulator looks at.

**Your Groq models.** Groq's deprecation page, as shown in a search summary, says llama-3.1-8b-instant and llama-3.3-70b-versatile were retired on 16 August 2026, and the Llama 4 Scout vision model in July ([Groq deprecations](https://console.groq.com/docs/deprecations)). If your app still calls these, it may already be failing. Check the Groq console today. Costs remain small: about USD 99 a month for 1,000 daily users making 20 calls each on Groq's cheaper model, using prices from a third-party tracker ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/groq-pricing)). The free tier supports a prototype, not 1,000 daily users.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The useful question is not "which app idea is most unique?" It is "who already pays, and can I reach them by hand?" In these notes, the only Indian buyers with visible prices are tutors, coaching centres and families at the counselling moment. Your study app is not wasted. It becomes the engine inside a tutor tool, and tutors can later bring students to the student app at no cost. One gap remains. Every documented Indian solo developer with good revenue sells business tools to global customers in dollars, but the notes contain no tested global idea for you. Revisit that path after the tutor test.

Start this week with three actions that cost nothing: check your Groq models, call 15 tutors, and check one real class test by hand. In the post-mortems the researchers found, the common cause of failure was no buyers, not bad code ([Collab365 Spaces](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/indie-saas-post-mortems-point-to-no-buyers-not-bad-code-utt3f3m2)). You can already build fast. The cheapest protection is to collect three to five payments before you write new code.
